<p class="rs-inbrief"><span class="rs-inbrief-label">In brief</span> Forty-eight Zen koans, short records of what teachers said and did, each followed by a comment and a four-line verse from the Chinese master Wumen, who gathered them in 1228. Still used in Rinzai Zen training. Here complete in Katsuki Sekida’s translation, with the Chinese.</p>

## Introduction

*The Gateless Gate* (in Chinese *Wumenguan*, in Japanese *Mumonkan*, 無門關) is a collection of forty-eight koans: brief exchanges between Zen teachers and their students, a question and an answer, a gesture, a story. The Chinese master Wumen Huikai (1183–1260), Mumon Ekai in Japanese, gathered them in the summer of 1228, when he was head monk of a monastery at Longxiang. He used them, his preface says, “as brickbats to batter the gate”. His name means “no gate”, so the title also reads as *Wumen’s Barrier*.

Each case comes in three parts: the case itself, often a few lines; Mumon’s comment, which prods and teases; and his four-line verse. The first is the best known. A monk asks Jōshū whether a dog has the Buddha Nature, and Jōshū answers: “Mu.” Mumon calls that one word “the front gate to Zen”.

The book dropped out of the Chinese Buddhist canon after the Song dynasty, but Wumen’s disciples took it to Japan, where it was reprinted many times. With the *Blue Cliff Record* it is still central to training in the Rinzai school.

The cases are not puzzles with answers in the back. Mumon’s own advice, in his postscript: “Your direct realization is demanded; it should not be sought through others.” One way to read them is one case at a time, the comment and verse with it, and then to leave it alone for a day. Sekida gives the names in their Japanese readings: Jōshū is the Chinese Zhaozhou, and Mumon is Wumen. The Chinese follows each part, in smaller type. The few notes are ours.

<h3 class="koan-title" id="preface"><a href="#preface">Mumon’s preface</a></h3>

<div class="koan-prose">

Buddhism makes mind its foundation and no-gate its gate. Now, how do you pass through this no-gate? It is said that things coming in through the gate can never be your own treasures. What is gained from external circumstances will perish in the end. However, such a saying is already raising waves when there is no wind. It is cutting unblemished skin. As for those who try to understand through other people’s words, they are striking at the moon with a stick; scratching a shoe, whereas it is the foot that itches. What concern have they with the truth?

<p class="orig" lang="zh">佛語心爲宗、無門爲法門。既是無門、且作麼生透。豈不見道、從門入者不是家珍、從縁得者始終成壞。恁麼説話、大似無風起浪好肉抉瘡。何況滯言句覓解會。掉棒打月、隔靴爬痒、有甚交渉。</p>

In the summer of the first year of Jōtei, Ekai was in Ryūshō Temple and as head monk worked with the monks, using the cases of the ancient masters as brickbats to batter the gate and lead them on according to their respective capacities. The text was written down not according to any scheme, but just to make a collection of forty-eight cases. It is called Mumonkan, “The Gateless Gate.”

<p class="orig" lang="zh">慧開、紹定戊子夏、首衆于東嘉龍翔。因納子請益、遂將古人公案作敲門瓦子、隨機引導學者。竟爾抄録、不覺成集。初不以前後敍列、共成四十八則。通曰無門關。</p>

A man of determination will unflinchingly push his way straight forward, regardless of all dangers. Then even the eight-armed Nata cannot hinder him. Even the four sevens of the West and the two threes of the East[^patriarchs] would beg for their lives. If one has no determination, then it will be like catching a glimpse of a horse galloping past the window: in the twinkling of an eye it will be gone.

<p class="orig" lang="zh">若是箇漢、不顧危亡單刀直入。八臂那咤、擱他不在。縦使西天四七、東土二三、只得望風乞命。設或躊躇、也似隔窓看馬騎、眨得眼來、早已蹉過。</p>

</div>

<div class="koan-verse rs-verse">

<p class="koan-label rs-label">Mumon’s verse <span lang="zh">頌曰</span></p>

The Great Way is gateless,  
Approached in a thousand ways.  
Once past this checkpoint  
You stride through the universe.

<p class="orig" lang="zh">大道無門<br />千差有路<br />透得此關<br />乾坤獨歩</p>

</div>

<h3 class="koan-title" id="1"><a href="#1"><span class="koan-num">Case 1</span> Jōshū’s “Mu” <span class="koan-title-zh" lang="zh">一　趙州狗子</span></a></h3>

<div class="koan-case">

A monk asked Jōshū, “Has a dog the Buddha Nature?” Jōshū answered, “Mu.”[^mu]

<p class="orig" lang="zh">趙州和尚、因僧問、狗子還有佛性也無。州云、無。</p>

</div>

<div class="koan-comment">

<p class="koan-label rs-label">Mumon’s comment</p>

In order to master Zen, you must pass the barrier of the patriarchs. To attain this subtle realization, you must completely cut off the way of thinking. If you do not pass the barrier, and do not cut off the way of thinking, then you will be like a ghost clinging to the bushes and weeds. Now, I want to ask you, what is the barrier of the patriarchs? Why, it is this single word “Mu.” That is the front gate to Zen. Therefore it is called the “Mumonkan of Zen.” If you pass through it, you will not only see Jōshū face to face, but you will also go hand in hand with the successive patriarchs, entangling your eyebrows with theirs, seeing with the same eyes, hearing with the same ears. Isn’t that a delightful prospect? Wouldn’t you like to pass this barrier?

<p class="orig" lang="zh">無門曰、參禪須透祖師關、妙悟要窮心路絶。祖關不透心路不絶、盡是依草附木精靈。且道、如何是祖師關。只者一箇無字、乃宗門一關也。遂目之曰禪宗無門關。透得過者、非但親見趙州、便可與歴代祖師把手共行、眉毛厮結同一眼見、同一耳聞。豈不慶快。莫有要透關底麼。</p>

Arouse your entire body with its three hundred and sixty bones and joints and its eighty-four thousand pores of the skin; summon up a spirit of great doubt and concentrate on this word “Mu.” Carry it continuously day and night. Do not form a nihilistic conception of vacancy, or a relative conception of “has” or “has not.” It will be just as if you swallow a red-hot iron ball, which you cannot spit out even if you try. All the illusory ideas and delusive thoughts accumulated up to the present will be exterminated, and when the time comes, internal and external will be spontaneously united. You will know this, but for yourself only, like a dumb man who has had a dream. Then all of a sudden an explosive conversion will occur, and you will astonish the heavens and shake the earth.

<p class="orig" lang="zh">將三百六十骨節、八萬四千毫竅、通身起箇疑團參箇無字。晝夜提撕、莫作虚無會、莫作有無會。如呑了箇熱鐵丸相似、吐又吐不出。蕩盡從前惡知惡覚、久久純熟自然内外打成一片、如唖子得夢、只許自知。驀然打發、驚天動地。</p>

It will be as if you snatch away the great sword of the valiant general Kan’u and hold it in your hand. When you meet the Buddha, you kill him; when you meet the patriarchs, you kill them. On the brink of life and death, you command perfect freedom; among the sixfold worlds and four modes of existence, you enjoy a merry and playful samadhi.[^samadhi]

<p class="orig" lang="zh">如奪得關將軍大刀入手、逢佛殺佛、逢祖殺祖、於生死岸頭得大自在、向六道四生中遊戲三昧。</p>

Now, I want to ask you again, “How will you carry it out?” Employ every ounce of your energy to work on this “Mu.” If you hold on without interruption, behold: a single spark, and the holy candle is lit!

<p class="orig" lang="zh">且作麼生提撕。盡平生氣力擧箇無字。若不間斷、好似法燭一點便著。</p>

</div>

<div class="koan-verse rs-verse">

<p class="koan-label rs-label">Mumon’s verse <span lang="zh">頌曰</span></p>

The dog, the Buddha Nature,  
The pronouncement, perfect and final.  
Before you say it has or has not,  
You are a dead man on the spot.

<p class="orig" lang="zh">狗子佛性<br />全提正令<br />纔渉有無<br />喪身失命</p>

</div>

<h3 class="koan-title" id="2"><a href="#2"><span class="koan-num">Case 2</span> Hyakujō’s Fox <span class="koan-title-zh" lang="zh">二　百丈野狐</span></a></h3>

<div class="koan-case">

When Hyakujō Oshō delivered a certain series of sermons, an old man always followed the monks to the main hall and listened to him. When the monks left the hall, the old man would also leave. One day, however, he remained behind, and Hyakujō asked him, “Who are you, standing here before me?” The old man replied. “I am not a human being. In the old days of Kashyapa Buddha, I was a head monk, living here on this mountain. One day a student asked me, ‘Does a man of enlightenment fall under the yoke of causation or not?’ I answered, ‘No, he does not.’ Since then I have been doomed to undergo five hundred rebirths as a fox. I beg you now to give the turning word to release me from my life as a fox. Tell me, does a man of enlightenment fall under the yoke of causation or not?” Hyakujō answered, “He does not ignore causation.” No sooner had the old man heard these words than he was enlightened. Making his bows, he said, “I am emancipated from my life as a fox. I shall remain on this mountain. I have a favor to ask of you: would you please bury my body as that of a dead monk.”

<p class="orig" lang="zh">百丈和尚、凡參次、有一老人常隨衆聽法。衆人退、老人亦退。忽一日不退。師遂問、面前立者復是何人。老人云、諾。某甲非人也。於過去迦葉佛時曾住此山。因學人問、大修行底人還落因果也無。某甲對云、不落因果。五百生墮野狐身。今請、和尚代一轉語貴脱野狐。遂問、大修行底人、還落因果也無。師云、不昧因果。老人於言下大悟。作禮云、某甲、已脱野狐身住在山後。敢告和尚。乞、依亡僧事例。</p>

Hyakujō had the director of the monks strike with the gavel and inform everyone that after the midday meal there would be a funeral service for a dead monk. The monks wondered at this, saying, “Everyone is in good health; nobody is in the sick ward. What does this mean?” After the meal Hyakujō led the monks to the foot of a rock on the far side of the mountain and with his staff poked out the dead body of a fox and performed the ceremony of cremation. That evening he ascended the rostrum and told the monks the whole story. Ōbaku thereupon asked him, “The old man gave the wrong answer and was doomed to be a fox for five hundred rebirths. Now, suppose he had given the right answer, what would have happened then?” Hyakujō said, “You come here to me, and I will tell you.” Ōbaku went up to Hyakujō and boxed his ears. Hyakujō clapped his hands with a laugh and exclaimed, “I was thinking that the barbarian had a red beard, but now I see before me the red-bearded barbarian himself.”

<p class="orig" lang="zh">師、令維那白槌告衆、食後送亡僧。大衆言議、一衆皆安、涅槃堂又無人病。何故如是。食後只見師領衆至山後嵒下、以杖挑出一死野狐、乃依火葬。師、至晩上堂、擧前因縁。黄蘗便問、古人錯祗對一轉語、墮五百生野狐身、轉轉不錯合作箇甚麼。師云、近前來與伊道。黄蘗遂近前、與師一掌。師拍手笑云、將謂、胡鬚赤。更有赤鬚胡。</p>

</div>

<div class="koan-comment">

<p class="koan-label rs-label">Mumon’s comment</p>

Not falling under causation: how could this make the monk a fox? Not ignoring causation: how could this make the old man emancipated? If you come to understand this, you will realize how old Hyakujō would have enjoyed five hundred rebirths as a fox.

<p class="orig" lang="zh">無門曰、不落因果、爲甚墮野狐。不昧因果、爲甚脱野狐。若向者裏著得一隻眼、便知得前百丈贏得風流五百生。</p>

</div>

<div class="koan-verse rs-verse">

<p class="koan-label rs-label">Mumon’s verse <span lang="zh">頌曰</span></p>

Not falling, not ignoring:  
Two faces of one die.  
Not ignoring, not falling:  
A thousand errors, a million mistakes.

<p class="orig" lang="zh">不落不昧<br />兩采一賽<br />不昧不落<br />千錯萬錯</p>

</div>

<h3 class="koan-title" id="3"><a href="#3"><span class="koan-num">Case 3</span> Gutei Raises a Finger <span class="koan-title-zh" lang="zh">三　倶胝竪指</span></a></h3>

<div class="koan-case">

Whenever Gutei Oshō was asked about Zen, he simply raised his finger. Once a visitor asked Gutei’s boy attendant, “What does your master teach?” The boy too raised his finger. Hearing of this, Gutei cut off the boy’s finger with a knife. The boy, screaming with pain, began to run away. Gutei called to him, and when he turned around, Gutei raised his finger. The boy suddenly became enlightened.

<p class="orig" lang="zh">倶胝和尚、凡有詰問、唯擧一指。後有童子。因外人問、和尚説何法要。童子亦竪指頭。胝聞遂以刃斷其指。童子、負痛號哭而去。胝復召之。童子廻首。胝却竪起指。童子忽然領悟。</p>

When Gutei was about to pass away, he said to his assembled monks, “I obtained one-finger Zen from Tenryū and used it all my life but still did not exhaust it.” When he had finished saying this, he entered into eternal Nirvana.

<p class="orig" lang="zh">胝將順世、謂衆曰、吾得天龍一指頭禪、一生受用不盡。言訖示滅。</p>

</div>

<div class="koan-comment">

<p class="koan-label rs-label">Mumon’s comment</p>

The enlightenment of Gutei and of the boy does not depend on the finger. If you understand this, Tenryū, Gutei, the boy, and you yourself are all run through with one skewer.

<p class="orig" lang="zh">無門曰、倶胝並童子悟處、不在指頭上。若向者裏見得、天龍同倶胝並童子與自己一串穿却。</p>

</div>

<div class="koan-verse rs-verse">

<p class="koan-label rs-label">Mumon’s verse <span lang="zh">頌曰</span></p>

Gutei made a fool of old Tenryū,  
Emancipating the boy with a single slice,  
Just as Kyorei cleaved Mount Kasan  
To let the Yellow River run through.

<p class="orig" lang="zh">倶胝鈍置老天龍<br />利刃單提勘小童<br />巨靈擡手無多子<br />分破華山千万重</p>

</div>

<h3 class="koan-title" id="4"><a href="#4"><span class="koan-num">Case 4</span> The Western Barbarian with No Beard <span class="koan-title-zh" lang="zh">四　胡子無髭</span></a></h3>

<div class="koan-case">

Wakuan said, “Why has the Western Barbarian no beard?”

<p class="orig" lang="zh">或庵曰、西天胡子、因甚無髭。</p>

</div>

<div class="koan-comment">

<p class="koan-label rs-label">Mumon’s comment</p>

Study should be real study, enlightenment should be real enlightenment. You should once meet this barbarian directly to be really intimate with him. But saying you are really intimate with him already divides you into two.

<p class="orig" lang="zh">無門曰、參須實參、悟須實悟。者箇胡子、直須親見一回始得。説親見、早成兩箇。</p>

</div>

<div class="koan-verse rs-verse">

<p class="koan-label rs-label">Mumon’s verse <span lang="zh">頌曰</span></p>

Don’t discuss your dream  
Before a fool.  
Barbarian with no beard  
Obscures the clarity.

<p class="orig" lang="zh">癡人面前<br />不可説夢<br />胡子髭無<br />惺惺添懵</p>

</div>

<h3 class="koan-title" id="5"><a href="#5"><span class="koan-num">Case 5</span> Kyōgen’s “Man up in a Tree” <span class="koan-title-zh" lang="zh">五　香嚴上樹</span></a></h3>

<div class="koan-case">

Kyōgen Oshō said, “It is like a man up in a tree hanging from a branch with his mouth; his hands grasp no bough, his feet rest on no limb. Someone appears under the tree and asks him, ‘What is the meaning of Bodhidharma’s coming from the West?’ If he does not answer, he fails to respond to the question. If he does answer, he will lose his life. What would you do in such a situation?”

<p class="orig" lang="zh">香嚴和尚云、如人上樹、口啣樹枝、手不攀枝、脚不踏樹。樹下有人問西來意、不對即違他所問、若對又喪身失命。正恁麼時、作麼生對。</p>

</div>

<div class="koan-comment">

<p class="koan-label rs-label">Mumon’s comment</p>

Even if your eloquence flows like a river, it is of no avail. Though you can expound the whole of Buddhist literature, it is of no use. If you solve this problem, you will give life to the way that has been dead until this moment and destroy the way that has been alive up to now. Otherwise you must wait for Maitreya Buddha and ask him.

<p class="orig" lang="zh">無門曰、縱有懸河之辨、惣用不著。説得一大藏教、亦用不著。若向者裏對得著、活却從前死路頭、死却從前活路頭。其或未然、直待當來問彌勒。</p>

</div>

<div class="koan-verse rs-verse">

<p class="koan-label rs-label">Mumon’s verse <span lang="zh">頌曰</span></p>

Kyōgen is truly thoughtless;  
His vice and poison are endless.  
He stops up the mouths of the monks,  
And devil’s eyes sprout from their bodies.

<p class="orig" lang="zh">香嚴眞杜撰<br />惡毒無盡限<br />唖却納僧口<br />通身迸鬼眼</p>

</div>

<h3 class="koan-title" id="6"><a href="#6"><span class="koan-num">Case 6</span> The Buddha Holds Out a Flower <span class="koan-title-zh" lang="zh">六　世尊拈花</span></a></h3>

<div class="koan-case">

When Shakyamuni Buddha was at Mount Grdhrakuta, he held out a flower to his listeners. Everyone was silent. Only Mahakashyapa broke into a broad smile. The Buddha said, “I have the True Dharma Eye, the Marvelous Mind of Nirvana, the True Form of the Formless, and the Subtle Dharma Gate, independent of words and transmitted beyond doctrine. This I have entrusted to Mahakashyapa.”

<p class="orig" lang="zh">世尊、昔、在靈山會上拈花示衆。是時、衆皆默然。惟迦葉尊者破顔微笑。世尊云、吾有正法眼藏、涅槃妙心、實相無相、微妙法門、不立文字、教外別傳、付囑摩訶迦葉。</p>

</div>

<div class="koan-comment">

<p class="koan-label rs-label">Mumon’s comment</p>

Golden-faced Gautama really disregarded his listeners. He made the good look bad and sold dog’s meat labeled as mutton. He himself thought it was wonderful. If, however, everyone in the audience had laughed, how could he have transmitted his True Eye? And again, if Mahakashyapa had not smiled, how could the Buddha have transmitted it? If you say the True Dharma Eye can be transmitted, then the golden-faced old man would be a city slicker who cheats the country bumpkin. If you say it cannot be transmitted, then why did the Buddha approve of Mahakashyapa?

<p class="orig" lang="zh">無門曰、黄面瞿曇、傍若無人。壓良爲賤、縣羊頭賣狗肉。將謂、多少奇特。只如當時大衆都笑、正法眼藏、作麼生傳。設使迦葉不笑、正法眼藏又作麼生傳。若道正法眼藏有傳授、黄面老子、誑謼閭閻。若道無傳授、爲甚麼獨許迦葉。</p>

</div>

<div class="koan-verse rs-verse">

<p class="koan-label rs-label">Mumon’s verse <span lang="zh">頌曰</span></p>

Holding out a flower,  
The Buddha betrayed his curly tail.  
Heaven and earth were bewildered,  
At Mahakashyapa’s smile.

<p class="orig" lang="zh">拈起花來<br />尾巴已露<br />迦葉破顔<br />人天罔措</p>

</div>

<h3 class="koan-title" id="7"><a href="#7"><span class="koan-num">Case 7</span> Jōshū’s “Wash Your Bowl” <span class="koan-title-zh" lang="zh">七　趙州洗鉢</span></a></h3>

<div class="koan-case">

A monk said to Jōshū, “I have just entered this monastery. Please teach me.” “Have you eaten your rice porridge?” asked Jōshū. “Yes, I have,” replied the monk. “Then you had better wash your bowl,” said Jōshū. With this the monk gained insight.

<p class="orig" lang="zh">趙州、因僧問、某甲乍入叢林。乞師指示。州云、喫粥了也未。僧云、喫粥了也。州云、洗鉢盂去。其僧有省。</p>

</div>

<div class="koan-comment">

<p class="koan-label rs-label">Mumon’s comment</p>

When he opens his mouth, Jōshū shows his gallbladder. He displays his heart and liver. I wonder if this monk really did hear the truth. I hope he did not mistake the bell for a jar.

<p class="orig" lang="zh">無門曰、趙州開口見膽、露出心肝。者僧聽事不眞、喚鐘作甕。</p>

</div>

<div class="koan-verse rs-verse">

<p class="koan-label rs-label">Mumon’s verse <span lang="zh">頌曰</span></p>

Endeavoring to interpret clearly,  
You retard your attainment.  
Don’t you know that flame is fire?  
Your rice has long been cooked.

<p class="orig" lang="zh">只爲分明極<br />翻令所得遲<br />早知燈是火<br />飯熟已多時</p>

</div>

<h3 class="koan-title" id="8"><a href="#8"><span class="koan-num">Case 8</span> Keichū the Wheelmaker <span class="koan-title-zh" lang="zh">八　奚仲造車</span></a></h3>

<div class="koan-case">

Gettan Oshō said, “Keichū, the first wheelmaker, made a cart whose wheels had a hundred spokes. Now, suppose you took a cart and removed both the wheels and the axle. What would you have?”

<p class="orig" lang="zh">月庵和尚問僧、奚仲造車一百輻。拈却兩頭、去却軸、明甚麼邊事。</p>

</div>

<div class="koan-comment">

<p class="koan-label rs-label">Mumon’s comment</p>

If anyone can directly master this topic, his eye will be like a shooting star, his spirit like a flash of lightning.

<p class="orig" lang="zh">無門曰、若也直下明得、眼、似流星、機、如掣電。</p>

</div>

<div class="koan-verse rs-verse">

<p class="koan-label rs-label">Mumon’s verse <span lang="zh">頌曰</span></p>

When the spiritual wheels turn,  
Even the master fails to follow them.  
They travel in all directions, above and below,  
North, south, east, and west.

<p class="orig" lang="zh">機輪轉處<br />達者猶迷<br />四維上下<br />南北東西</p>

<h3 class="koan-title" id="9"><a href="#9"><span class="koan-num">Case 9</span> Daitsū Chishō Buddha <span class="koan-title-zh" lang="zh">九　大通智勝</span></a></h3>

<div class="koan-case">

A monk asked Kōyō Seijō, “Daitsū Chishō Buddha sat in zazen for ten kalpas and could not attain Buddhahood. He did not become a Buddha. How could this be?” Seijō said, “Your question is quite self-explanatory.” The monk asked, “He meditated so long; why could he not attain Buddhahood?” Seijō said, “Because he did not become a Buddha.”

<p class="orig" lang="zh">興陽讓和尚、因僧問、大通智勝佛、十劫坐道場、佛法不現前、不得成佛道時如何。讓曰、其問甚諦當。僧云、既是坐道場、爲甚麼不得成佛道。讓曰、爲伊不成佛。</p>

</div>

<div class="koan-comment">

<p class="koan-label rs-label">Mumon’s comment</p>

I allow the barbarian’s realization, but I do not allow his understanding. When an ignorant man realizes it, he is a sage. When a sage understands it, he is ignorant.

<p class="orig" lang="zh">無門曰、只許老胡知、不許老胡會。凡夫若知、即是聖人。聖人若會、即是凡夫。</p>

</div>

<div class="koan-verse rs-verse">

<p class="koan-label rs-label">Mumon’s verse <span lang="zh">頌曰</span></p>

Better emancipate your mind than your body;  
When the mind is emancipated, the body is free,  
When both body and mind are emancipated,  
Even gods and spirits ignore worldly power.

<p class="orig" lang="zh">了身何似了心休<br />了得心兮身不愁<br />若也身心倶了了<br />神仙何必更封侯</p>

</div>

<h3 class="koan-title" id="10"><a href="#10"><span class="koan-num">Case 10</span> Seizei Is Utterly Destitute <span class="koan-title-zh" lang="zh">十　清税孤貧</span></a></h3>

<div class="koan-case">

Seizei said to Sōzan, “Seizei is utterly destitude. Will you give him support?” Sōzan called out, “Seizei!” Seizei responded, “Yes, sir!” Sōzan said, “You have finished three cups of the finest wine in China, and still you say you have not yet moistened your lips!”

<p class="orig" lang="zh">曹山和尚、因僧問云、清税孤貧。乞、師賑濟。山云、税闍梨。税、應諾。山曰、青原白家酒、三盞喫了、猶道未沾唇。</p>

</div>

<div class="koan-comment">

<p class="koan-label rs-label">Mumon’s comment</p>

Seizei pretended to retreat. What was his scheme? Sōzan had the eye of Buddha and saw through his opponent’s motive. However, I want to ask you, at what point did Seizei drink wine?

<p class="orig" lang="zh">無門曰、清税輸機、是何心行。曹山具眼、深辨來機。然雖如是、且道、那裏是税闍梨、喫酒處。</p>

</div>

<div class="koan-verse rs-verse">

<p class="koan-label rs-label">Mumon’s verse <span lang="zh">頌曰</span></p>

Poverty like Hantan’s,  
Mind like Kōu’s;  
With no means of livelihood,  
He dares to rival the richest.

<p class="orig" lang="zh">貧似范丹<br />氣如項羽<br />活計雖無<br />敢與鬪富</p>

</div>

<h3 class="koan-title" id="11"><a href="#11"><span class="koan-num">Case 11</span> Jōshū Sees the Hermits <span class="koan-title-zh" lang="zh">十一　州勘庵主</span></a></h3>

<div class="koan-case">

Jōshū went to a hermit’s cottage and asked, “Is the master in? Is the master in?” The hermit raised his fist. Jōshū said, “The water is too shallow to anchor here,” and he went away. Coming to another hermit’s cottage, he asked again, “Is the master in? Is the master in?” This hermit, too, raised his fist. Jōshū said, “Free to give, free to take, free to kill, free to save,” and he made a deep bow.

<p class="orig" lang="zh">趙州、到一庵主處問、有麼有麼。主、竪起拳頭。州云、水淺不是泊舡處。便行。又到一庵主處云、有麼有麼。主亦竪起拳頭。州云、能縱能奪、能殺能活。便作禮。</p>

</div>

<div class="koan-comment">

<p class="koan-label rs-label">Mumon’s comment</p>

Both raised their fists; why was the one accepted and the other rejected? Tell me, what is the difficulty here? If you can give a turning word to clarify this problem, you will realize that Jōshū’s tongue has no bone in it, now helping others up, now knocking them down, with perfect freedom. However, I must remind you: the two hermits could also see through Jōshū. If you say there is anything to choose between the two hermits, you have no eye of realization. If you say there is no choice between the two, you have no eye of realization.

<p class="orig" lang="zh">無門曰、一般竪起拳頭、爲甚麼肯一箇、不肯一箇。且道、誵訛在甚處。若向者裏下得一轉語、便見趙州舌頭無骨、扶起放倒、得大自在。雖然如是爭奈、趙州却被二庵主勘破。若道二庵主有優劣、未具參學眼。若道無優劣、亦未具參學眼。</p>

</div>

<div class="koan-verse rs-verse">

<p class="koan-label rs-label">Mumon’s verse <span lang="zh">頌曰</span></p>

The eye like a shooting star,  
The spirit like a lighting;  
A death-dealing blade,  
A life-giving sword.

<p class="orig" lang="zh">眼流星<br />機掣電<br />殺人刀<br />活人劍</p>

</div>

<h3 class="koan-title" id="12"><a href="#12"><span class="koan-num">Case 12</span> Zuigan Calls His Master <span class="koan-title-zh" lang="zh">十二　巖喚主人</span></a></h3>

<div class="koan-case">

Zuigan Gen Oshō called to himself every day, “Master!” and answered, “Yes, sir!” Then he would say, “Be wide awake!” and answer, “Yes, sir!” “Henceforward, never be deceived by others!” “No, I won’t!”

<p class="orig" lang="zh">瑞巖彦和尚、毎日自喚主人公、復自應諾。乃云、惺惺着。喏。他時異日、莫受人瞞。喏喏。</p>

</div>

<div class="koan-comment">

<p class="koan-label rs-label">Mumon’s comment</p>

Old Zuigan buys and sells himself. He takes out a lot of god-masks and devil-masks and puts them on and plays with them. What for, eh? One calling and the other answering; one wide awake, the other saying he will never be deceived. If you stick to any of them, you will be a failure. If you imitate Zuigan, you will play the fox.

<p class="orig" lang="zh">無門曰、瑞巖老子、自買自賣、弄出許多神頭鬼面。何故。𦗚。一箇喚底、一箇應底。一箇惺惺底、一箇不受人瞞底。認着依前還不是。若也傚他、惣是野狐見解。</p>

</div>

<div class="koan-verse rs-verse">

<p class="koan-label rs-label">Mumon’s verse <span lang="zh">頌曰</span></p>

Clinging to the deluded way of consciousness,  
Students of the Way do not realize truth.  
The seed of birth and death through endless eons:  
The fool calls it the true original self.

<p class="orig" lang="zh">學道之人不識眞<br />只爲從前認識神<br />無量劫來生死本<br />癡人喚作本來人</p>

</div>

<h3 class="koan-title" id="13"><a href="#13"><span class="koan-num">Case 13</span> Tokusan Holds His Bowls <span class="koan-title-zh" lang="zh">十三　徳山托鉢</span></a></h3>

<div class="koan-case">

One day Tokusan went down toward the dining room, holding his bowls. Seppō met him and asked, “Where are you off to with your bowls? The bell has not rung, and the drum has not sounded.” Tokusan turned and went back to his room. Seppō mentioned this to Gantō, who remarked, “Tokusan is renowned, but he does not know the last word.” Tokusan heard about this remark and sent his attendant to fetch Gantō. “You do not approve of me?” he asked. Gantō whispered his meaning. Tokusan said nothing at the time, but the next day he ascended the rostrum, and behold! he was very different from usual! Gantō, going toward the front of the hall, clapped his hands and laughed loudly, saying, “Congratulations! Our old man has got hold of the last word! From now on, nobody in this whole country can outdo him!”

<p class="orig" lang="zh">徳山、一日托鉢下堂。見雪峰問者老漢鐘未鳴鼓未響、托鉢向甚處去、山便回方丈。峰擧似巖頭。頭云、大小徳山未會末後句。山聞令侍者喚巖頭來、問曰、汝不肯老僧那。巖頭密啓其意。山乃休去。明日陞座、果與尋常不同。巖頭至僧堂前、拊掌大笑云、且喜得老漢會末後句。他後天下人、不奈伊何。</p>

</div>

<div class="koan-comment">

<p class="koan-label rs-label">Mumon’s comment</p>

As for the last word, neither Gantō nor Tokusan has ever dreamed of it! When you look into the matter, you find they are like puppets on the shelf!

<p class="orig" lang="zh">無門曰、若是末後句、巖頭徳山倶未夢見在。撿點將來、好似一棚傀儡。</p>

</div>

<div class="koan-verse rs-verse">

<p class="koan-label rs-label">Mumon’s verse <span lang="zh">頌曰</span></p>

If you realize the first,  
You master the last.  
The first and the last  
Are not one word.

<p class="orig" lang="zh">識得最初句<br />便會末後句<br />末後與最初<br />不是者一句</p>

</div>

<h3 class="koan-title" id="14"><a href="#14"><span class="koan-num">Case 14</span> Nansen Cuts the Cat in Two <span class="koan-title-zh" lang="zh">十四　南泉斬猫</span></a></h3>

<div class="koan-case">

Nansen Oshō saw monks of the Eastern and Western halls quarreling over a cat. He held up the cat and said, “If you can give an answer, you will save the cat. If not, I will kill it.” No one could answer, and Nansen cut the cat in two. That evening Jōshū returned, and Nansen told him of the incident. Jōshū took off his sandal, placed it on his head, and walked out. “If you had been there, you would have saved the cat,” Nansen remarked.

<p class="orig" lang="zh">南泉和尚因東西堂爭猫兒。泉乃提起云、大衆道得即救、道不得即斬却也。衆無對。泉遂斬之。晩趙州外歸。泉擧似州。州乃脱履安頭上而出。泉云、子若在即救得猫兒。</p>

</div>

<div class="koan-comment">

<p class="koan-label rs-label">Mumon’s comment</p>

Tell me, what did Jōshū mean when he put the sandal on his head? If you can give a turning word on this, you will see that Nansen’s decree was carried out with good reason. If not, “Danger!”

<p class="orig" lang="zh">無門曰、且道、趙州頂草鞋意作麼生。若向者裏下得一轉語、便見南泉令不虚行。其或未然險。</p>

</div>

<div class="koan-verse rs-verse">

<p class="koan-label rs-label">Mumon’s verse <span lang="zh">頌曰</span></p>

Had Jōshū been there,  
He would have done the opposite;  
When the sword is snatched away,  
Even Nansen begs for his life.

<p class="orig" lang="zh">趙州若在<br />倒行此令<br />奪却刀子<br />南泉乞命</p>

</div>

<h3 class="koan-title" id="15"><a href="#15"><span class="koan-num">Case 15</span> Tōzan’s Sixty Blows <span class="koan-title-zh" lang="zh">十五　洞山三頓</span></a></h3>

<div class="koan-case">

Tōzan came to study with Unmon. Unmon asked, “Where are you from?” “From Sato,” Tōzan replied. “Where were you during the summer?” “Well, I was at the monastery of Hōzu, south of the lake.” “When did you leave there,” Unmon asked. “On August 25” was Tōzan’s reply. “I spare you sixty blows,” Unmon said. The next day Tōzan came to Unmon and said, “Yesterday you said you spared me sixty blows. I beg to ask you, where was I at fault?” “Oh, you rice bag!” shouted Unmon. “What makes you wander about, now west of the river, now south of the lake?” Tōzan thereupon came to a mighty enlightenment experience.

<p class="orig" lang="zh">雲門、因洞山參次、門問曰、近離甚處。山云、査渡。門曰、夏在甚處。山云、湖南報慈。門曰、幾時離彼。山云、八月二十五。門曰、放汝三頓棒。山至明日却上問訊。昨日蒙和尚放三頓棒。不知過在甚麼處。門曰、飯袋子、江西湖南便恁麼去。山於此大悟。</p>

</div>

<div class="koan-comment">

<p class="koan-label rs-label">Mumon’s comment</p>

If Unmon had given Tōzan the true food of Zen and encouraged him to develop an active Zen spirit, his school would not have declined as it did. Tōzan had an agonizing struggle through the whole night, lost in the sea of right and wrong. He reached a complete impasse. After waiting for the dawn, he again went to Unmon, and Unmon again made him a picture book of Zen. Even though he was directly enlightened, Tōzan could not be called brilliant. Now, I want to ask you, should Tōzan have been given sixty blows or not? If you say yes, you admit that all the universe should be beaten. If you say no, then you accuse Unmon of telling a lie. If you really understand the secret, you will be able to breathe out Zen spirit with the very mouth of Tōzan.

<p class="orig" lang="zh">無門曰、雲門、當時便與本分草料、使洞山別有生機一路、家門不致寂寥。一夜在是非海裏著到、直待天明再來、又與他注破。洞山直下悟去、未是性燥。且問諸人、洞山三頓棒、合喫不合喫。若道合喫、草木叢林皆合喫棒。若道不合喫、雲門又成誑語。向者裏明得、方與洞山出一口氣。</p>

</div>

<div class="koan-verse rs-verse">

<p class="koan-label rs-label">Mumon’s verse <span lang="zh">頌曰</span></p>

The lion had a secret to puzzle his cub;  
The cub crouched, leaped, and dashed forward.  
The second time, a casual move led to checkmate.  
The first arrow was light, but the second went deep.

<p class="orig" lang="zh">獅子教兒迷子訣<br />擬前跳躑早翻身<br />無端再敍當頭著<br />前箭猶輕後箭深</p>

</div>

<h3 class="koan-title" id="16"><a href="#16"><span class="koan-num">Case 16</span> When the Bell Sounds <span class="koan-title-zh" lang="zh">十六　鐘聲七條</span></a></h3>

<div class="koan-case">

Unmon said, “The world is vast and wide. Why do you put on your seven-piece robe at the sound of the bell?”

<p class="orig" lang="zh">雲門曰、世界恁麼廣闊。因甚向鐘聲裏披七條。</p>

</div>

<div class="koan-comment">

<p class="koan-label rs-label">Mumon’s comment</p>

In studying Zen, you should not be swayed by sounds and forms. Even though you attain insight when hearing a voice or seeing a form, this is simply the ordinary way of things. Don’t you know that the real Zen student commands sounds, controls forms, is clear-sighted at every event and free on every occasion? Granted you are free, just tell me: Does the sound come to the ear or does the ear go to the sound? If both sound and silence die away, at such a juncture how could you talk of Zen? While listening with you ear, you cannot tell. When hearing with your eye, you are truly intimate.

<p class="orig" lang="zh">無門曰、大凡參禪學道、切忌、隨聲逐色。縱使聞聲悟道、見色明心也是尋常。殊不知、納僧家、騎聲蓋色、頭頭上明、著著上妙。然雖如是。且道、聲來耳畔、耳往聲邊。直饒響寂雙忘、到此如何話會。若將耳聽應難會、眼處聞聲方始親。</p>

</div>

<div class="koan-verse rs-verse">

<p class="koan-label rs-label">Mumon’s verse <span lang="zh">頌曰</span></p>

With realization, things make one family;  
Without realization, things are separated in a thousand ways.  
Without realization, things make one family;  
With realization, things are separated in a thousand ways.

<p class="orig" lang="zh">會則事同一家<br />不會萬別千差<br />不會事同一家<br />會則萬別千差</p>

</div>

<h3 class="koan-title" id="17"><a href="#17"><span class="koan-num">Case 17</span> Chū the National Teacher Gives Three Calls <span class="koan-title-zh" lang="zh">十七　國師三喚</span></a></h3>

<div class="koan-case">

The National Teacher called his attendant three times, and three times the attendant responded. The National Teacher said, “I long feared that I was betraying you, but really it was you who were betraying me.”

<p class="orig" lang="zh">國師三喚侍者。侍者三應。國師云、將謂吾辜負汝、元來却是汝辜負吾。</p>

</div>

<div class="koan-comment">

<p class="koan-label rs-label">Mumon’s comment</p>

The National Teacher called three times, and his tongue fell to the ground. The attendant responded three times, and he gave his answer with brilliance. The National Teacher was old and lonely; he held the cow’s head and forced it to eat grass. The attendant would have none of it; delicious food has little attraction for a man who is satiated. Tell me, at what point was the betrayal? When the country is flourishing, talent is prized. When the home is wealthy, the children are proud.

<p class="orig" lang="zh">無門曰、國師三喚、舌頭墮地。侍者三應、和光吐出。國師年老心孤、按牛頭喫草。侍者未肯承當。美食不中飽人飡、且道、那裏是他辜負處。國淨才子貴、家富小兒嬌。</p>

</div>

<div class="koan-verse rs-verse">

<p class="koan-label rs-label">Mumon’s verse <span lang="zh">頌曰</span></p>

He carried and iron yoke with no hole  
And left a curse to trouble his descendants.  
If you want to hold up the gate and the doors,  
You must climb a mountain of swords with bare feet.

<p class="orig" lang="zh">鉄枷無孔要人擔<br />累及兒孫不等閑<br />欲得撑門并拄戸<br />更須赤脚上刀山</p>

</div>

<h3 class="koan-title" id="18"><a href="#18"><span class="koan-num">Case 18</span> Tōzan’s “Masagin” <span class="koan-title-zh" lang="zh">十八　洞山三斤</span></a></h3>

<div class="koan-case">

A monk asked Tōzan, “What is Buddha?” Tōzan replied, “Masagin!” [three pounds of flax].

<p class="orig" lang="zh">洞山和尚、因僧問、如何是佛。山云、麻三斤。</p>

</div>

<div class="koan-comment">

<p class="koan-label rs-label">Mumon’s comment</p>

Old Tōzan attained the poor Zen of a clam. He opened the two halves of the shell a little and exposed all the liver and intestines inside. But tell me, how do you see Tōzan?

<p class="orig" lang="zh">無門曰、洞山老人、參得些蚌蛤禪、纔開兩片露出肝腸。然雖如是、且道、向甚處見洞山。</p>

</div>

<div class="koan-verse rs-verse">

<p class="koan-label rs-label">Mumon’s verse <span lang="zh">頌曰</span></p>

“Three pounds of flax” came sweeping along;  
Close were the words, but closer was the meaning.  
Those who argue about right and wrong  
Are those enslaved by right and wrong.

<p class="orig" lang="zh">突出麻三斤<br />言親意更親<br />來説是非者<br />便是是非人</p>

</div>

<h3 class="koan-title" id="19"><a href="#19"><span class="koan-num">Case 19</span> Nansen’s “Ordinary Mind Is the Way” <span class="koan-title-zh" lang="zh">十九　平常是道</span></a></h3>

<div class="koan-case">

Jōshū asked Nansen, “What is the Way?” “Ordinary mind is the Way,” Nansen replied. “Shall I try to seek after it?” Jōshū asked. “If you try for it, you will become separated from it,” responded Nansen. “How can I know the Way unless I try for it?” persisted Jōshū. Nansen said, “The Way is not a matter of knowing or not knowing. Knowing is delusion; not knowing is confusion. When you have really reached the true Way beyond doubt, you will find it as vast and boundless as outer space. How can it be talked about on the level of right and wrong?” With these words, Jōshū came to a sudden realization.

<p class="orig" lang="zh">南泉、因趙州問、如何是道。泉云、平常心是道。州云、還可趣向否。泉云、擬向即乖。州云、不擬爭知是道。泉云、道不屬知、不屬不知。知是妄覺、不知是無記。若眞達不擬之道、猶如太虚廓然洞豁。豈可強是非也。州於言下頓悟。</p>

</div>

<div class="koan-comment">

<p class="koan-label rs-label">Mumon’s comment</p>

Nansen dissolved and melted away before Jōshū’s question, and could not offer a plausible explanation. Even though Jōshū comes to a realization, he must delve into it for another thirty years before he can fully understand it.

<p class="orig" lang="zh">無門曰、南泉被趙州發問、直得瓦解氷消、分疎不下。趙州縱饒悟去、更參三十年始得。</p>

</div>

<div class="koan-verse rs-verse">

<p class="koan-label rs-label">Mumon’s verse <span lang="zh">頌曰</span></p>

The spring flowers, the autumn moon;  
Summer breezes, winter snow.  
If useless things do not clutter your mind,  
You have the best days of your life.

<p class="orig" lang="zh">春有百花秋有月<br />夏有涼風冬有雪<br />若無閑事挂心頭<br />更是人間好時節</p>

</div>

<h3 class="koan-title" id="20"><a href="#20"><span class="koan-num">Case 20</span> The Man of Great Strength <span class="koan-title-zh" lang="zh">二十　大力量人</span></a></h3>

<div class="koan-case">

Shōgen Oshō asked, “Why is it that a man of great strength does not lift his legs?” And he also said, “It is not the tongue he speaks with.”

<p class="orig" lang="zh">松源和尚云、大力量人、因甚擡脚不起。又云、開口不在舌頭上。</p>

</div>

<div class="koan-comment">

<p class="koan-label rs-label">Mumon’s comment</p>

It must be said that Shōgen shows us all his stomach and intestines. But alas, no one can appreciate him! And even if someone could appreciate him, let him come to me, and I’ll beat him severely. Why? If you want to find pure gold, you must see it through fire.

<p class="orig" lang="zh">無門曰、松源可謂、傾腸倒腹。只是欠人承當。縱饒直下承當、正好來無門處喫痛棒。何故。𦗚。要識眞金、火裏看。</p>

</div>

<div class="koan-verse rs-verse">

<p class="koan-label rs-label">Mumon’s verse <span lang="zh">頌曰</span></p>

Lifting his leg, he kicks up the Scented Ocean;  
Lowering his head, he looks down on the fourth Dhyana heaven.  
There is no space vast enough for his body—  
Now, somebody write the last line here.

<p class="orig" lang="zh">擡脚踏翻香水海<br />低頭俯視四禪天<br />一箇渾身無處著請<br />續一句</p>

</div>

<h3 class="koan-title" id="21"><a href="#21"><span class="koan-num">Case 21</span> Unmon’s “Kanshiketsu” <span class="koan-title-zh" lang="zh">二十一　雲門屎橛</span></a></h3>

<div class="koan-case">

A monk asked Unmon, “What is Buddha?” Unmon replied, “Kanshiketsu!” [A dry shit-stick.]

<p class="orig" lang="zh">雲門、因僧問、如何是佛。門云、乾屎橛。</p>

</div>

<div class="koan-comment">

<p class="koan-label rs-label">Mumon’s comment</p>

Unmon was too poor to prepare plain food, too busy to speak from notes. He hurriedly took up shiketsu to support the Way. The decline of Buddhism was thus foreshadowed.

<p class="orig" lang="zh">無門曰、雲門可謂、家貧難辨素食、事忙不及草書。動便將屎橛來、撑門拄戸。佛法興衰可見。</p>

</div>

<div class="koan-verse rs-verse">

<p class="koan-label rs-label">Mumon’s verse <span lang="zh">頌曰</span></p>

Lightning flashing,  
Sparks shooting;  
A moment’s blinking,  
Missed forever.

<p class="orig" lang="zh">閃電光<br />撃石火<br />眨得眼<br />已蹉過</p>

</div>

<h3 class="koan-title" id="22"><a href="#22"><span class="koan-num">Case 22</span> Kashyapa’s “Knock Down the Flagpole” <span class="koan-title-zh" lang="zh">二十二　迦葉刹竿</span></a></h3>

<div class="koan-case">

Ananda asked Kashyapa, “The World-honored One gave you the golden robe; did he give you anything else?” “Ananda!” cried Kashyapa. “Yes, sir!” answered Ananda. “Knock down the flagpole at the gate,” said Kashyapa.

<p class="orig" lang="zh">迦葉、因阿難問云、世尊傳金襴袈裟外、別傳何物。葉喚云、阿難。難、應諾。葉云、倒却門前刹竿著。</p>

</div>

<div class="koan-comment">

<p class="koan-label rs-label">Mumon’s comment</p>

If you can give a turning word at this point, you will see that the meeting at Mount Grdhrakuta is still solemnly continuing. If not, then this is what Vipasyin Buddha worried about from remote ages; up to now he has still not acquired the essence.

<p class="orig" lang="zh">無門曰、若向者裏下得一轉語親切、便見靈山一會儼然未散。其或未然、毘婆尸佛、早留心、直至而今不得妙。</p>

</div>

<div class="koan-verse rs-verse">

<p class="koan-label rs-label">Mumon’s verse <span lang="zh">頌曰</span></p>

Tell me—question or answer—which was more intimate?  
Many have knit their brows over this;  
Elder brother calls, younger brother answers, and they betray the family secret.  
They had a special spring, not one of yin and yang.

<p class="orig" lang="zh">問處何如答處親<br />幾人於此眼生筋<br />兄呼弟應揚家醜<br />不屬陰陽別是春</p>

</div>

<h3 class="koan-title" id="23"><a href="#23"><span class="koan-num">Case 23</span> Think Neither Good Nor Evil <span class="koan-title-zh" lang="zh">二十三　不思善惡</span></a></h3>

<div class="koan-case">

The Sixth Patriarch was pursued by the monk Myō as far as Taiyu Mountain. The patriarch, seeing Myō coming, laid the robe and bowl on a rock and said, “This robe represents the faith; it should not be fought over. If you want to take it away, take it now.” Myō tried to move it, but it was as heavy as a mountain and would not budge. Faltering and trembling, he cried out, “I came for the Dharma, not for the robe. I beg you, please give me your instruction.” The patriarch said, “Think neither good nor evil. At this very moment, what is the original self of the monk Myō?” At these words, Myō was directly illuminated. His whole body was covered with sweat. He wept and bowed, saying, “Besides the secret words and the secret meaning you have just now revealed to me, is there anything else, deeper still?” The patriarch said, “What I have told you is no secret at all. When you look into your own true self, whatever is deeper is found right there.” Myō said, “I was with the monks under Ōbai for many years but I could not realize my true self. But now, receiving your instruction, I know it is like a man drinking water and knowing whether it is cold or warm. My lay brother, you are now my teacher.” The patriarch said, “If you say so, but let us both call Ōbai our teacher. Be mindful to treasure and hold fast to what you have attained.”

<p class="orig" lang="zh">六祖、因明上座、趁至大庾嶺。祖見明至、即擲衣鉢於石上云、此衣表信。可力爭耶、任君將去。明遂擧之如山不動、踟蹰悚慄。明曰、我來求法、非爲衣也。願行者開示。祖云、不思善、不思惡、正與麼時、那箇是明上座本來面目。明當下大悟、遍體汗流。泣涙作禮、問曰、上來密語密意外、還更有意旨否。祖曰、我今爲汝説者、即非密也。汝若返照自己面目、密却在汝邊。明云、某甲雖在黄梅隨衆、實未省自己面目。今蒙指授入處、如人飲水冷暖自知。今行者即是某甲師也。祖云、汝若如是則吾與汝同師黄梅。善自護持。</p>

</div>

<div class="koan-comment">

<p class="koan-label rs-label">Mumon’s comment</p>

The Sixth Patriarch was, so to speak, hurried into helping a man in an emergency, and he displayed a grandmotherly kindness. It is as though he peeled a fresh lichi, removed the seed, put it in your mouth, and asked you to swallow it down.

<p class="orig" lang="zh">無門曰、六祖可謂、是事出急家老婆心切。譬如新茘支剥了殻去了核、送在你口裏、只要你嚥一嚥。</p>

</div>

<div class="koan-verse rs-verse">

<p class="koan-label rs-label">Mumon’s verse <span lang="zh">頌曰</span></p>

You cannot describe it; you cannot picture it;  
You cannot admire it; don’t try to eat it raw.  
Your true self has nowhere to hide;  
When the world is destroyed, it is not destroyed.

<p class="orig" lang="zh">描不成兮畫不就<br />贊不及兮休生受<br />本來面目没處藏<br />世界壞時渠不朽</p>

</div>

<h3 class="koan-title" id="24"><a href="#24"><span class="koan-num">Case 24</span> Fuketsu’s Speech and Silence <span class="koan-title-zh" lang="zh">二十四　離却語言</span></a></h3>

<div class="koan-case">

A monk asked Fuketsu, “Both speech and silence are faulty in being ri [<span lang="zh">離</span> inward action of mind] or bi [<span lang="zh">微</span> outward action of mind]. How can we escape these faults?”

Fuketsu said,  
“I always remember the spring in Kōnan,  
Where the partridges sing;  
How fragrant the countless flowers!”

<p class="orig" lang="zh">風穴和尚、因僧問、語默渉離微、如何通不犯。穴云、長憶江南三月裏、鷓鴣啼處百花香。</p>

</div>

<div class="koan-comment">

<p class="koan-label rs-label">Mumon’s comment</p>

Fuketsu’s Zen spirit was like lightning and opened a clear passage.

However, he was entangled in the monk’s words and could not cut them off.  
If you can really grasp the problem, you can readily find the way out.  
Now, putting language samadhi aside, say it in your own words.

<p class="orig" lang="zh">無門曰、風穴機如掣電得路便行。爭奈坐前人舌頭不斷。若向者裏見得親切、自有出身之路。且離却語言三昧、道將一句來。</p>

</div>

<div class="koan-verse rs-verse">

<p class="koan-label rs-label">Mumon’s verse <span lang="zh">頌曰</span></p>

He does not use a refined phrase;  
Before speaking, he has already handed it over.  
If you chatter on and on,  
You will find you have lost your way.

<p class="orig" lang="zh">不露風骨句<br />未語先分付<br />進歩口喃喃<br />知君大罔措</p>

</div>

<h3 class="koan-title" id="25"><a href="#25"><span class="koan-num">Case 25</span> Kyōzan’s Dream <span class="koan-title-zh" lang="zh">二十五　三座説法</span></a></h3>

<div class="koan-case">

In a dream Kyōzan Oshō went to Maitreya’s place and was led in to sit in the third seat. A senior monk struck with a gavel and said, “Today the one in the third seat will speak.” Kyōzan rose and, striking with the gavel, said, “The truth of Mahayana is beyond the four propositions and transcends the hundred negations. Taichō! Taichō!” [Hear the truth!]

<p class="orig" lang="zh">仰山和尚、夢見往彌勒所、安第三座。有一尊者、白槌云、今日當第三座説法。山乃起白槌云、摩訶衍法離四句、絶百非。諦聽、諦聽。</p>

</div>

<div class="koan-comment">

<p class="koan-label rs-label">Mumon’s comment</p>

Now tell me, did Kyōzan preach or did he not not? If he opens his mouth, he is lost; if he seals his mouth, he is lost. Even if he neither opens nor shuts his mouth, he is a hundred and eight thousand [miles away from the truth].

<p class="orig" lang="zh">無門曰、且道、是説法不説法、開口即失、閉口又喪。不開不閉、十万八千。</p>

</div>

<div class="koan-verse rs-verse">

<p class="koan-label rs-label">Mumon’s verse <span lang="zh">頌曰</span></p>

In broad daylight, under the blue sky,  
He forges a dream in a dream;  
He makes up a monstrous story  
And tries to deceive the whole crowd.

<p class="orig" lang="zh">白日晴天<br />夢中説夢<br />捏怪捏怪<br />誑謼一衆</p>

</div>

<h3 class="koan-title" id="26"><a href="#26"><span class="koan-num">Case 26</span> Two Monks Roll Up the Blinds <span class="koan-title-zh" lang="zh">二十六　二僧卷簾</span></a></h3>

<div class="koan-case">

When the monks assembled before the midday meal to listen to his lecture, the great Hōgen of Seiryō pointed at the bamboo blinds. Two monks simultaneously went and rolled them up. Hōgen said, “One gain, one loss.”

<p class="orig" lang="zh">清涼大法眼、因僧齋前上參。眼以手指簾。時有二僧、同去卷簾。眼曰、一得一失。</p>

</div>

<div class="koan-comment">

<p class="koan-label rs-label">Mumon’s comment</p>

Tell me, who gained and who lost? If you have an eye to penetrate the secret, you will see where Seiryō Kokushi failed. However, I warn you strongly against discussing gain and loss.

<p class="orig" lang="zh">無門曰、且道、是誰得誰失。若向者裏著得一隻眼、便知清涼國師敗闕處。然雖如是、切忌向得失裏商量。</p>

</div>

<div class="koan-verse rs-verse">

<p class="koan-label rs-label">Mumon’s verse <span lang="zh">頌曰</span></p>

Rolling up the blinds, the great sky is open,  
But the great sky does not come up to Zen.  
Why don’t you throw them all down from the sky,  
And keep your practice so close that no air can escape?

<p class="orig" lang="zh">卷起明明徹太空<br />太空猶未合吾宗<br />爭似從空都放下<br />綿綿密密不通風</p>

</div>

<h3 class="koan-title" id="27"><a href="#27"><span class="koan-num">Case 27</span> Nansen’s “Not Mind, Not Buddha, Not Things” <span class="koan-title-zh" lang="zh">二十七　不是心佛</span></a></h3>

<div class="koan-case">

A monk asked Nansen, “Is there any Dharma that has not been preached to the people?” Nansen answered, “There is.” “What is the truth that has not been taught?” asked the monk. Nansen said, “It is not mind; it is not Buddha; it is not things.”

<p class="orig" lang="zh">南泉和尚、因僧問云、還有不與人説底法麼。泉云、有。僧云、如何是不與人説底法。泉云、不是心、不是佛、不是物。</p>

</div>

<div class="koan-comment">

<p class="koan-label rs-label">Mumon’s comment</p>

At this question, Nansen used up all his treasure and was not a little confused.

<p class="orig" lang="zh">無門曰、南泉被者一問、直得揣盡家私、郎當不少。</p>

</div>

<div class="koan-verse rs-verse">

<p class="koan-label rs-label">Mumon’s verse <span lang="zh">頌曰</span></p>

Talking too much spoils your virtue;  
Silence is truly unequaled.  
Let the mountains become the sea;  
I’ll give you no comment.

<p class="orig" lang="zh">叮嚀損君徳<br />無言眞有功<br />任從滄海變<br />終不爲君通</p>

</div>

<h3 class="koan-title" id="28"><a href="#28"><span class="koan-num">Case 28</span> Ryūtan Blows Out the Candle <span class="koan-title-zh" lang="zh">二十八　久嚮龍潭</span></a></h3>

<div class="koan-case">

Tokusan asked Ryūtan about Zen far into the night. At last Ryūtan said, “The night is late. Why don’t you retire?” Tokusan made his bows and lifted the blinds to withdraw, but he was met by darkness. Turning back to Ryūtan, he said, “It is dark outside.” Ryūtan lit a paper candle and handed it to him. Tokusan was about to take it when Ryūtan blew it out. At this, all of a sudden, Tokusan went through a deep experience and made bows. Ryūtan said, “What sort of realization do you have?” “From now on,” said Tokusan, “I will not doubt the words of an old oshō who is renowned everywhere under the sun.” The next day Ryūtan ascended the rostrum and said, “I see a fellow among you. His fangs are like the sword tree. His mouth is like a blood bowl. Strike him with a stick, and he won’t turn his head to look at you. Someday or other, he will climb the highest of the peaks and establish our Way there.” Tokusan brought his notes on the Diamond Sutra to the front of the hall, pointed to them with a torch, and said, “Even though you have exhausted the abtruse doctrines, it is like placing a hair in a vast space. Even though you have learned all the secrets of the world, it is like a drop of water dripped on the great ocean.” And he burned all his notes. Then, making bows, he took his leave of his teacher.

<p class="orig" lang="zh">龍潭、因徳山請益抵夜。潭云、夜深。子何不下去。山遂珍重掲簾而出。見外面黒却回云、外面黒。潭乃點紙燭度與。山擬接。潭便吹滅。山於此忽然有省。便作禮。潭云、子見箇甚麼道理。山云、某甲從今日去不疑天下老和尚舌頭也。至明日、龍潭陞堂云、可中有箇漢、牙如劍樹、口似血盆。一棒打不回頭。他時異日、向孤峰頂上立吾道在。山遂取疏抄於法堂前、將一炬火提起云、窮諸玄辨、若一毫致於太虚、竭世樞機似一滴投於巨壑。將疏抄便燒。於是禮辭。</p>

</div>

<div class="koan-comment">

<p class="koan-label rs-label">Mumon’s comment</p>

Before Tokusan crossed the barrier from his native place, his mind burned and his mouth uttered bitterness. He went soutward, intending to stamp out the doctrines of special transmission outside the sutras. When he reached the road to Reishū, he asked an old woman to let him have lunch to “refresh the mind.” “Your worship, what sort of literature do you carry in your pack?” the old woman asked. “Commentaries on the Diamond Sutra,” replied Tokusan. The old woman said, “I hear it is said in that sutra, ‘The past mind cannot be held, the present mind cannot be held, the future mind cannot be held.’ Now, I would like to ask you, what mind are you going to have refreshed?” At this question Tokusan was dumbfounded. However, he did not remain inert under her words but asked, “Do you know of any good teacher around here?” The old woman said, “Five miles from here you will find Ryūtan Oshō.” Coming to Ryūtan, Tokusan got the worst of it. His former words were inconsistent with his later ones. As for Ryūtan, he seemed to have lost all sense of shame in his compassion toward his son. Finding a bit of live coal in the other, enough to start a fire, he hurriedly poured on muddy water to annihilate everything at once. A little cool reflection tells us it was all a farce.

<p class="orig" lang="zh">無門曰、徳山未出關時、心憤憤、口悱悱、得得來南方要滅却教外別傳之旨。及到澧州路上問婆子買點心。婆云、大徳車子内是甚麼文字。山云、金剛經疏抄。婆云、只如經中道、過去心不可得、見在心不可得、未來心不可得。大徳、要點那箇心。徳山被者一問、直得口似匾檐。然雖如是、未肯向婆子句下死却。遂問婆子、近處有甚麼宗師。婆云、五里外有龍潭和尚。及到龍潭納盡敗闕。可謂是前言不應後語。龍潭大似憐兒不覺醜。見他有些子火種、郎忙將惡水驀頭一澆澆殺。冷地看來、一場好笑。</p>

</div>

<div class="koan-verse rs-verse">

<p class="koan-label rs-label">Mumon’s verse <span lang="zh">頌曰</span></p>

Hearing the name cannot surpass seeing the face;  
Seeing the face cannot surpass hearing the name.  
He may have saved his nose,  
But alas! he lost his eyes.

<p class="orig" lang="zh">聞名不如見面<br />見面不如聞名<br />雖然救得鼻孔<br />爭奈瞎却眼睛</p>

</div>

<h3 class="koan-title" id="29"><a href="#29"><span class="koan-num">Case 29</span> The Sixth Patriarch’s “Your Mind Moves” <span class="koan-title-zh" lang="zh">二十九　非風非幡</span></a></h3>

<div class="koan-case">

The wind was flapping a temple flag, and two monks started an argument. One said the flag moved, the other said the wind moved; they argued back and forth but could not reach a conclusion. The Sixth Patriarch said, “It is not the wind that moves, it is not the flag that moves; it is your mind that moves.” The two monks were awe-struck.

<p class="orig" lang="zh">六祖、因風颺刹幡。有二僧、對論。一云、幡動。一云、風動。往復曾未契理。祖云、不是風動、不是幡動、仁者心動。二僧悚然。</p>

</div>

<div class="koan-comment">

<p class="koan-label rs-label">Mumon’s comment</p>

It is not the wind that moves; it is not the flag that moves; it is not the mind that moves. How do you see the patriarch? If you come to understand this matter deeply, you will see that the two monks got gold when buying iron. The patriarch could not withhold his compassion and courted disgrace.

<p class="orig" lang="zh">無門曰、不是風動、不是幡動、不是心動、甚處見祖師。若向者裏見得親切、方知二僧買鐵得金。祖師忍俊不禁、一場漏逗。</p>

</div>

<div class="koan-verse rs-verse">

<p class="koan-label rs-label">Mumon’s verse <span lang="zh">頌曰</span></p>

Wind, flag, mind, moving,  
All equally to blame.  
Only knowing how to open his mouth,  
Unaware of his fault in talking.

<p class="orig" lang="zh">風幡心動<br />一状領過<br />只知開口<br />不覺話墮</p>

</div>

<h3 class="koan-title" id="30"><a href="#30"><span class="koan-num">Case 30</span> Baso’s “This Very Mind Is the Buddha” <span class="koan-title-zh" lang="zh">三十　即心即佛</span></a></h3>

<div class="koan-case">

Daibai asked Baso, “What is the Buddha?” Baso answered, “This very mind is the Buddha.”

<p class="orig" lang="zh">馬祖、因大梅問、如何是佛。祖云、即心是佛。</p>

</div>

<div class="koan-comment">

<p class="koan-label rs-label">Mumon’s comment</p>

If you directly grasp Baso’s meaning, you wear the Buddha’s clothes, eat the Buddha’s food, speak the Buddha’s words, do the Buddha’s deeds—that is, you are a Buddha himself. However, alas! Daibai misled not a few people into taking the mark on the balance for the weight itself. How could he realize that even mentioning the word “Buddha” should make us rinse out our mouths for three days? If a man of understanding hears anyone say, “This very mind is the Buddha,” he will cover his ears and rush away.

<p class="orig" lang="zh">無門曰、若能直下領略得去、著佛衣、喫佛飯、説佛話、行佛行、即是佛也。然雖如是、大梅引多少人、錯認定盤星。爭知道説箇佛字、三日漱口。若是箇漢、見説即心是佛、掩耳便走。</p>

</div>

<div class="koan-verse rs-verse">

<p class="koan-label rs-label">Mumon’s verse <span lang="zh">頌曰</span></p>

The blue sky and bright day,  
No more searching around!  
“What is the Buddha?” you ask:  
With loot in your pocket, you declare yourself innocent.

<p class="orig" lang="zh">青天白日<br />切忌尋覓<br />更問如何<br />抱贓叫屈</p>

</div>

<h3 class="koan-title" id="31"><a href="#31"><span class="koan-num">Case 31</span> Jōshū Investigates an Old Woman <span class="koan-title-zh" lang="zh">三十一　趙州勘婆</span></a></h3>

<div class="koan-case">

A monk asked an old woman, “What is the way to Taisan?” The old woman said, “Go straight on.” When the monk had proceeded a few steps, she said, “A good, respectable monk, but he too goes that way.” Afterward someone told Jōshū about this. Jōshū said, “Wait a bit, I will go and investigate the old woman for you.” The next day he went and asked the same question, and the old woman gave the same answer. On returning, Jōshū said to his disciples, “I have investigated the old woman of Taisan for you.”

<p class="orig" lang="zh">趙州、因僧問婆子、臺山路向甚處去。婆云、驀直去。僧纔行三五歩。婆云、好箇師僧、又恁麼去。後有僧擧似州。州云、待我去與你勘過這婆子。明日便去亦如是問。婆亦如是答。州歸謂衆曰、臺山婆子、我與爾勘破了也。</p>

</div>

<div class="koan-comment">

<p class="koan-label rs-label">Mumon’s comment</p>

The old woman only knew how to sit still in her tent and plan the campaign; she did not know when she was shadowed by a spy. Though old Jōshū showed himself clever enough to take a camp and overwhelm a fortress, he displayed no trace of being a great commander. If we look at them, they both have their faults. But tell me, what did Jōshū see in the old woman?

<p class="orig" lang="zh">無門曰、婆子只解坐籌帷幄、要且著賊不知。趙州老人、善用偸營劫塞乃機、又且無大人相。撿點將來、二倶有過。且道、那裏是趙州、勘破婆子處。</p>

</div>

<div class="koan-verse rs-verse">

<p class="koan-label rs-label">Mumon’s verse <span lang="zh">頌曰</span></p>

The question was like the others,  
The answer was the same.  
Sand in the rice,  
Thorns in the mud.

<p class="orig" lang="zh">問既一般<br />答亦相似<br />飯裏有砂<br />泥中有刺</p>

</div>

<h3 class="koan-title" id="32"><a href="#32"><span class="koan-num">Case 32</span> A Non-Buddhist Philosopher Questions the Buddha <span class="koan-title-zh" lang="zh">三十二　外道問佛</span></a></h3>

<div class="koan-case">

A non-Buddhist philosopher said to the Buddha, “I do not ask for words; I don not ask for non-words.” The Buddha just sat there. The philosopher said admiringly, “The World-honored One, with his great mercy, has blown away the clouds of my illusion and enabled me to enter the Way.” And after making bows, he took his leave. Then Ananda asked the Buddha, “What did he realize, to admire you so much?” The World-honored One replied, “A fine horse runs even at the shadow of the whip.”

<p class="orig" lang="zh">世尊、因外道問、不問有言、不問無言。世尊據座。外道贊歎云、世尊大慈大悲、開我迷雲令我得入。乃具禮而去。阿難尋問佛、外道有何所證贊歎而去。世尊云、如世良馬見鞭影而行。</p>

</div>

<div class="koan-comment">

<p class="koan-label rs-label">Mumon’s comment</p>

Ananda was the Buddha’s disciple, but his understanding was not equal to that of the non-Buddhist. I want to ask you, what difference is there between the Buddha’s disciple and the non-Buddhist?

<p class="orig" lang="zh">無門曰、阿難乃佛弟子、宛不如外道見解、且道、外道與佛弟子相去多少。</p>

</div>

<div class="koan-verse rs-verse">

<p class="koan-label rs-label">Mumon’s verse <span lang="zh">頌曰</span></p>

On the edge of a sword,  
Over the ridge of an iceberg,  
With no steps, no ladders,  
Climbing the cliffs without hands.

<p class="orig" lang="zh">劍刃上行<br />氷稜上走<br />不渉階梯<br />懸崖撒手</p>

</div>

<h3 class="koan-title" id="33"><a href="#33"><span class="koan-num">Case 33</span> Baso’s “No Mind, No Buddha” <span class="koan-title-zh" lang="zh">三十三　非心非佛</span></a></h3>

<div class="koan-case">

A monk asked Baso, “What is the Buddha?” Baso answered, “No mind, no Buddha.”

<p class="orig" lang="zh">馬祖、因僧問、如何是佛。祖曰、非心非佛。</p>

</div>

<div class="koan-comment">

<p class="koan-label rs-label">Mumon’s comment</p>

If you understand this, you have finished studying Zen.

<p class="orig" lang="zh">無門曰、若向者裏見得、參學事畢。</p>

</div>

<div class="koan-verse rs-verse">

<p class="koan-label rs-label">Mumon’s verse <span lang="zh">頌曰</span></p>

Present a sword if you meet a swordsman;  
Don’t offer a poem unless you meet a poet.  
When talking, tell one-third of it;  
Don’t divulge the whole at once.

<p class="orig" lang="zh">路逢劍客須呈<br />不遇詩人莫獻<br />逢人且説三分<br />未可全施一片</p>

</div>

<h3 class="koan-title" id="34"><a href="#34"><span class="koan-num">Case 34</span> Nansen’s “Reason Is Not the Way” <span class="koan-title-zh" lang="zh">三十四　智不是道</span></a></h3>

<div class="koan-case">

Nansen said, “Mind is not the Buddha, reason is not the Way.”

<p class="orig" lang="zh">南泉云、心不是佛、智不是道。</p>

</div>

<div class="koan-comment">

<p class="koan-label rs-label">Mumon’s comment</p>

Nansen, growing old, had no shame. Just opening his stinking mouth, he let slip the family secrets. Yet there are very few who are grateful for his kindness.

<p class="orig" lang="zh">無門曰、南泉可謂、老不識羞。纔開臭口、家醜外揚。然雖如是、知恩者少。</p>

</div>

<div class="koan-verse rs-verse">

<p class="koan-label rs-label">Mumon’s verse <span lang="zh">頌曰</span></p>

The sky clears, the sun shines bright,  
The rain comes, the earth gets wet.  
He opens his heart and expounds the whole secret,  
But I fear he is little appreciated.

<p class="orig" lang="zh">天晴日頭出<br />雨下地上濕<br />盡情都説了<br />只恐信不及</p>

</div>

<h3 class="koan-title" id="35"><a href="#35"><span class="koan-num">Case 35</span> Seijō’s Soul Separated <span class="koan-title-zh" lang="zh">三十五　倩女離魂</span></a></h3>

<div class="koan-case">

Goso said to his monks, “Seijō’s soul separated from her being. Which was the real Seijō?”

<p class="orig" lang="zh">五祖問僧云、倩女離魂、那箇是眞底。</p>

</div>

<div class="koan-comment">

<p class="koan-label rs-label">Mumon’s comment</p>

When you realize what the real is, you will see that we pass from one husk to another like travelers stopping for a night’s lodging. But if you do not realize it yet, I earnestly advise you not to rush about wildly. When earth, water, fire, and air suddenly separate, you will be like a crab struggling in boiling water with its seven or eight arms and legs. When that happens, don’t say I didn’t warn you!

<p class="orig" lang="zh">無門曰、若向者裏悟得眞底、便知出殻入殻如宿旅舎。其或未然、切莫亂走。驀然地水火風一散、如落湯螃蟹七手八脚。那時莫言、不道。</p>

</div>

<div class="koan-verse rs-verse">

<p class="koan-label rs-label">Mumon’s verse <span lang="zh">頌曰</span></p>

The moon above the clouds is ever the same;  
Valleys and mountains are separate from each other.  
All are blessed, all are blessed;  
Are they one or are they two?

<p class="orig" lang="zh">雲月是同<br />渓山各異<br />萬福萬福<br />是一是二</p>

</div>

<h3 class="koan-title" id="36"><a href="#36"><span class="koan-num">Case 36</span> When You Meet a Man of the Way <span class="koan-title-zh" lang="zh">三十六　路逢達道</span></a></h3>

<div class="koan-case">

Goso said, “When you meet a man of the Way on the path, do not meet him with words or in silence. Tell me, how will you meet him?”

<p class="orig" lang="zh">五祖曰、路逢達道人、不將語默對、且道、將甚麼對。</p>

</div>

<div class="koan-comment">

<p class="koan-label rs-label">Mumon’s comment</p>

In such a case, if you can manage an intimate meeting with him it will certainly be gratifying. But if you cannot, you must be watchful in every way.

<p class="orig" lang="zh">無門曰、若向者裏對得親切、不妨慶快。其或未然、也須一切處著眼。</p>

</div>

<div class="koan-verse rs-verse">

<p class="koan-label rs-label">Mumon’s verse <span lang="zh">頌曰</span></p>

Meeting a man of the Way on the road,  
Meet him with neither words nor silence.  
A punch on the jaw:  
Understand, if you can directly understand.

<p class="orig" lang="zh">路逢達道人<br />不將語默對<br />攔腮劈面拳<br />直下會便會</p>

</div>

<h3 class="koan-title" id="37"><a href="#37"><span class="koan-num">Case 37</span> Jōshū’s Oak Tree <span class="koan-title-zh" lang="zh">三十七　庭前栢樹</span></a></h3>

<div class="koan-case">

A monk asked Jōshū, “What is the meaning of Bodhidharma’s coming to China?” Jōshū said, “The oak tree in the garden.”

<p class="orig" lang="zh">趙州、因僧問、如何是祖師西來意。州云、庭前栢樹子。</p>

</div>

<div class="koan-comment">

<p class="koan-label rs-label">Mumon’s comment</p>

If you understand Jōshū’s answer intimately, there is no Shakya before you, no Maitreya to come.

<p class="orig" lang="zh">無門曰、若向趙州答處見得親切、前無釋迦後無彌勒。</p>

</div>

<div class="koan-verse rs-verse">

<p class="koan-label rs-label">Mumon’s verse <span lang="zh">頌曰</span></p>

Words cannot express things;  
Speech does not convey the spirit.  
Swayed by words, one is lost;  
Blocked by phrases, one is belwildered.

<p class="orig" lang="zh">言無展事<br />語不投機<br />承言者喪<br />滯句者迷</p>

</div>

<h3 class="koan-title" id="38"><a href="#38"><span class="koan-num">Case 38</span> A Buffalo Passes the Window <span class="koan-title-zh" lang="zh">三十八　牛過窓櫺</span></a></h3>

<div class="koan-case">

Goso said, “A buffalo passes by the window. His head, horns, and four legs all go past. But why can’t the tail pass too?”

<p class="orig" lang="zh">五祖曰、譬如水牯牛過窓櫺、頭角四蹄都過了、因甚麼尾巴過不得。</p>

</div>

<div class="koan-comment">

<p class="koan-label rs-label">Mumon’s comment</p>

If you make a complete about-face, open your eye, and give a turning word on this point, you will be able to repay the four kinds of love that have favored you and help the sentient beings in the three realms who follow you. If you are still unable to do this, return to this tail and reflect upon it, and then for the first time you will realize something.

<p class="orig" lang="zh">無門曰、若向者裏顛倒、著得一隻眼、下得一轉語、可以上報四恩下資三有。其或未然、更須照顧尾巴始得。</p>

</div>

<div class="koan-verse rs-verse">

<p class="koan-label rs-label">Mumon’s verse <span lang="zh">頌曰</span></p>

Passing by, it falls into a ditch;  
Coming back, all the worse, it is lost.  
This tiny little tail,  
What a strange thing it is!

<p class="orig" lang="zh">過去墮坑塹<br />回來却被壞<br />者些尾巴子<br />直是甚奇怪</p>

</div>

<h3 class="koan-title" id="39"><a href="#39"><span class="koan-num">Case 39</span> A Mistake in Speaking <span class="koan-title-zh" lang="zh">三十九　雲門話墮</span></a></h3>

<div class="koan-case">

A monk said to Unmon, “The brilliance of the Buddha silently illuminates the whole universe. . .” But before the could finish the verse, Unmon said, “Aren’t those the words of Chōetsu the Genius?” “Yes, they are,” answered the monk. “You have slipped up in your speaking,” Unmon said. Afterward, Shishin Zenji brought up the matter and said, “Tell me, at what point did the monk err in his speaking?”

<p class="orig" lang="zh">雲門、因僧問、光明寂照遍河沙。一句未絶、門遽曰、豈不是張拙秀才語。僧云、是。門云、話墮也。後來、死心拈云、且道、那裏是者僧話墮處。</p>

</div>

<div class="koan-comment">

<p class="koan-label rs-label">Mumon’s comment</p>

If you clearly understand this and realize how exacting Unmon was in his method, and what made the monk err in his speaking, you are qualified to be a teacher of heaven and earth. If you are not yet clear about it, you are far from saving yourself.

<p class="orig" lang="zh">無門曰、若向者裏見得雲門用處孤危、者僧因甚話墮、堪與人天爲師。若也未明、自救不了。</p>

</div>

<div class="koan-verse rs-verse">

<p class="koan-label rs-label">Mumon’s verse <span lang="zh">頌曰</span></p>

A line cast in the rapids,  
The greedy will be caught.  
Before you start to open your mouth,  
Your life is already lost!

<p class="orig" lang="zh">急流垂釣<br />貪餌者著<br />口縫纔開<br />性命喪却</p>

</div>

<h3 class="koan-title" id="40"><a href="#40"><span class="koan-num">Case 40</span> Tipping Over a Water Bottle <span class="koan-title-zh" lang="zh">四十　趯倒淨瓶</span></a></h3>

<div class="koan-case">

When Isan Oshō was with Hyakujō, he was tenzo [<span lang="zh">典座</span> head cook] of the monastery. Hyakujō wanted to choose a master for Mount Tai-i, so he called together all the monks and told them that anyone who could answer his question in an outstanding manner would be chosen. Then he took a water bottle and stood it on the floor, and said, “You may not call this a water bottle. What do you call it?” The head monk said, “It cannot be called a stump.” Hyakujō asked Isan his opinion. Isan tipped over the water bottle with his feet and went out. Hyakujō laughed and said, “The head monk loses.” And Isan was named as the founder of the new monastery.

<p class="orig" lang="zh">溈山和尚、始在百丈會中充典座。百丈、將選大溈主人。乃請同首座對衆下語、出格者可往。百丈遂拈淨瓶、置地上設問云、不得喚作淨瓶、汝喚作甚麼。首座乃云、不可喚作木𣔻也。百丈却問於山。山乃趯倒淨瓶而去。百丈笑云、第一座輸却山子也。因命之爲開山。</p>

</div>

<div class="koan-comment">

<p class="koan-label rs-label">Mumon’s comment</p>

Isan displayed great spirit in his action, but he could not cut himself free from Hyakujō’s apron strings. He preferred the heavier task to lighter one. Why was he like that, eh? He took off his headband to bear the iron yoke.

<p class="orig" lang="zh">無門曰、溈山一期之勇、爭奈跳百丈圈圚不出、撿點將來、便重不便輕。何故。𦗚。脱得盤頭、擔起鐵枷。</p>

</div>

<div class="koan-verse rs-verse">

<p class="koan-label rs-label">Mumon’s verse <span lang="zh">頌曰</span></p>

Tossing bamboo baskets and ladles away,  
He made a glorious dash and swept all before him.  
Hyakujō’s barrier cannot stop his advance;  
Thousands of Buddhas come forth from the tips of his feet.

<p class="orig" lang="zh">颺下笊籬并木杓<br />當陽一突絶周遮<br />百丈重關攔不住<br />脚尖趯出佛如麻</p>

</div>

<h3 class="koan-title" id="41"><a href="#41"><span class="koan-num">Case 41</span> Bodhidharma’s Mind-Pacifying <span class="koan-title-zh" lang="zh">四十一　達磨安心</span></a></h3>

<div class="koan-case">

Bodhidharma sat facing the wall. The Second Patriarch stood in the snow. He cut off his arm and presented it to Bodhidharma, crying, “My mind has no peace as yet! I beg you, master, please pacify my mind!” “Bring your mind here and I will pacify it for you,” replied Bodhidharma. “I have searched for my mind, and I cannot take hold of it,” said the Second Patriarch. “Now your mind is pacified,” said Bodhidharma.

<p class="orig" lang="zh">達磨面壁。二祖立雪。斷臂云、弟子心未安、乞師安心。磨云、將心來爲汝安。祖云、覓心了不可得。磨云、爲汝安心竟。</p>

</div>

<div class="koan-comment">

<p class="koan-label rs-label">Mumon’s comment</p>

The broken-toothed old Hindu came so importantly, thousands of miles over the sea. This was raising waves where there was no wind. In his last years he induced enlightenment in his disciple, who, to make matters worse, was defective in the six roots. Why, Shasanro did not know for ideographs.

<p class="orig" lang="zh">無門曰、缺齒老胡、十万里航海特特而來。可謂是無風起浪。末後接得一箇門人、又却六根不具。咦。謝三郎不識四字。</p>

</div>

<div class="koan-verse rs-verse">

<p class="koan-label rs-label">Mumon’s verse <span lang="zh">頌曰</span></p>

Coming east, directly pointing,  
You entrusted the Dharma, and trouble arose;  
The clamor of the monasteries  
Is all because of you.

<p class="orig" lang="zh">西來直指<br />事因囑起<br />撓聒叢林<br />元來是爾</p>

</div>

<h3 class="koan-title" id="42"><a href="#42"><span class="koan-num">Case 42</span> The Girl Comes out of Samadhi <span class="koan-title-zh" lang="zh">四十二　女子出定</span></a></h3>

<div class="koan-case">

Once, in the old days, in the time of the World-honored One, Manjusri went to the assembly of the Buddhas and found that everyone had departed to his original dwelling place. Only a girl remained, sitting in samadhi close to the Buddha’s throne. Manjusri asked Shakyamuni Buddha, “Why can the girl get near the Buddha’s throne, while I cannot?” Shakyamuni Buddha said, “Bring her out of her samadhi and ask her yourself.” Manjusri walked around the girl three times, snapped his fingers once, took her to the Brahma heaven, and exerted all his miraculous powers to bring her out of her meditation, but in vain.

<p class="orig" lang="zh">世尊、昔、因文殊、至諸佛集處値諸佛各還本處。惟有一女人近彼佛坐入於三昧。文殊乃白佛、云何女人得近佛坐而我不得。佛告文殊、汝但覺此女、令從三昧起、汝自問之。文殊遶女人三匝、鳴指一下、乃托至梵天盡其神力而不能出。</p>

The World-honored One said, “Even a hundred thousand Manjusris cannot make her wake up. But down below, past twelve hundred million lands as innumerable as the sands of Ganges, there is a Bodhisattva Mōmyō. He will be able to rouse her from her samadhi.” Instantly the Bodhisattva Mōmyō emerged from the earth and made a bow to the World-honored One, who gave him his imperial order. The Bodhisattva went over to the girl and snapped his fingers once. At this she came out of her samadhi.

<p class="orig" lang="zh">世尊云、假使百千文殊亦出此女人定不得。下方過一十二億河沙國土有罔明菩薩。能出此女人定。須臾罔明大士、從地湧出禮拜世尊。世尊敕罔明。却至女人前鳴指一下。女人於是從定而出。</p>

</div>

<div class="koan-comment">

<p class="koan-label rs-label">Mumon’s comment</p>

Old Shakyamuni put a petty drama on the stage and failed to enlighten the masses. I want to ask you: Manjusri is the teacher of the Seven Buddhas; why couldn’t he arouse the girl from her samadhi? How was it that Mōmyō, a Bodhisattva at the beginner’s stage, could do it? If you understand this intimately, you will enjoy Nagya’s grand samadhi in the busiest activity of consciousness.

<p class="orig" lang="zh">無門曰、釋迦老子、做者一場雜劇、不通小小。且道、文殊是七佛之師、因甚出女人定不得。罔明初地菩薩、爲甚却出得。若向者裏見得親切、業識忙忙那伽大定。</p>

</div>

<div class="koan-verse rs-verse">

<p class="koan-label rs-label">Mumon’s verse <span lang="zh">頌曰</span></p>

One was successful, the other was not;  
Both secured freedom of mind.  
One in a god-mask, the other in a devil-mask;  
Even in defeat, a beautiful performance.

<p class="orig" lang="zh">出得出不得<br />渠儂得自由<br />神頭并鬼面<br />敗闕當風流</p>

</div>

<h3 class="koan-title" id="43"><a href="#43"><span class="koan-num">Case 43</span> Shuzan’s Shippei <span class="koan-title-zh" lang="zh">四十三　首山竹篦</span></a></h3>

<div class="koan-case">

Shuzan Oshō held up his shippei [staff of office] before his disciples and said, “You monks! If you call this a shippei, you oppose its reality. If you do not call it a shippei, you ignore the fact. Tell me, you monks, what will you call it?”

<p class="orig" lang="zh">首山和尚、拈竹篦示衆云、汝等諸人、若喚作竹篦則觸。不喚作竹篦則背。汝諸人、且道、喚作甚麼。</p>

</div>

<div class="koan-comment">

<p class="koan-label rs-label">Mumon’s comment</p>

If you call it a shippei, you oppose its reality. If you do not call it a shippei, you ignore the fact. Words are not available; silence is not available. Now, tell me quickly, what is it?

<p class="orig" lang="zh">無門曰、喚作竹篦則觸。不喚作竹篦則背。不得有語、不得無語。速道、速道。</p>

</div>

<div class="koan-verse rs-verse">

<p class="koan-label rs-label">Mumon’s verse <span lang="zh">頌曰</span></p>

Holding up the shippei,  
He takes life, he gives life.  
Opposing and ignoring interweave.  
Even Buddhas and patriarchs beg for their lives.

<p class="orig" lang="zh">拈起竹篦<br />行殺活令<br />背觸交馳<br />佛祖乞命</p>

</div>

<h3 class="koan-title" id="44"><a href="#44"><span class="koan-num">Case 44</span> Bashō’s Staff <span class="koan-title-zh" lang="zh">四十四　芭蕉拄杖</span></a></h3>

<div class="koan-case">

Bashō Osho said to his disciples, “If you have a staff, I will give you a staff. If you have no staff, I will take it from you.”

<p class="orig" lang="zh">芭蕉和尚示衆云、你有拄杖子、我與你拄杖子。你無拄杖子、我奪你拄杖子。</p>

</div>

<div class="koan-comment">

<p class="koan-label rs-label">Mumon’s comment</p>

It helps me wade across a river when the bridge is down. It accompanies me to the village on a moonless night. If you call it a staff, you will enter hell like an arrow.

<p class="orig" lang="zh">無門曰、扶過斷橋水、伴歸無月村。若喚作拄杖、入地獄如箭。</p>

</div>

<div class="koan-verse rs-verse">

<p class="koan-label rs-label">Mumon’s verse <span lang="zh">頌曰</span></p>

The depths and shallows of the world  
Are all in its grasp.  
It supports the heaven and sustains the earth.  
Everywhere, it enhances the doctrine.

<p class="orig" lang="zh">諸方深與淺<br />都在掌握中<br />撑天并拄地<br />隨處振宗風</p>

</div>

<h3 class="koan-title" id="45"><a href="#45"><span class="koan-num">Case 45</span> Hōen’s “Who Is He?” <span class="koan-title-zh" lang="zh">四十五　他是阿誰</span></a></h3>

<div class="koan-case">

Hōen of Tōzan said, “Even Shakya and Maitreya are servants of another. I want to ask you, who is he?”

<p class="orig" lang="zh">東山演師祖曰、釋迦彌勒猶是他奴。且道、他是阿誰。</p>

</div>

<div class="koan-comment">

<p class="koan-label rs-label">Mumon’s comment</p>

If you can really see this “another” with perfect clarity, it is like encountering your own father at a crossroads. Why should you ask whether you recognize him or not?

<p class="orig" lang="zh">無門曰、若也見得他分曉、譬如十字街頭撞見親爺相似、更不須問別人道是與不是。</p>

</div>

<div class="koan-verse rs-verse">

<p class="koan-label rs-label">Mumon’s verse <span lang="zh">頌曰</span></p>

Don’t draw another’s bow,  
Don’t ride another’s horse,  
Don’t discuss another’s faults,  
Don’t explore another’s affairs.

<p class="orig" lang="zh">他弓莫挽<br />他馬莫騎<br />他非莫辨<br />他事莫知</p>

</div>

<h3 class="koan-title" id="46"><a href="#46"><span class="koan-num">Case 46</span> Proceed On from the Top of the Pole <span class="koan-title-zh" lang="zh">四十六　竿頭進歩</span></a></h3>

<div class="koan-case">

Sekisō Oshō asked, “How can you proceed on further from the top of a hundred-foot pole?” Another eminent teacher of old said, “You, who sit on the top of a hundred-foot pole, although you have entered the Way you are not yet genuine. Proceed on from the top of the pole, and you will show your whole body in the ten directions.”

<p class="orig" lang="zh">石霜和尚云、百尺竿頭、如何進歩。又古徳云、百尺竿頭坐底人、雖然得入未爲眞。百尺竿頭、須進歩十方世界現全身。</p>

</div>

<div class="koan-comment">

<p class="koan-label rs-label">Mumon’s comment</p>

If you go on further and turn your body about, no place is left where you are not the master. But even so, tell me, how will you go on further from the top of a hundred-foot pole? Eh?”

<p class="orig" lang="zh">無門曰、進得歩、翻得身、更嫌何處不稱尊。然雖如是、且道、百尺竿頭、如何進歩。嗄。</p>

</div>

<div class="koan-verse rs-verse">

<p class="koan-label rs-label">Mumon’s verse <span lang="zh">頌曰</span></p>

He darkens the third eye of insight  
And clings to the first mark on the scale.  
Even though he may sacrifice his life,  
He is only a blind man leading the blind.

<p class="orig" lang="zh">瞎却頂門眼<br />錯認定盤星<br />拌身能捨命<br />一盲引衆盲</p>

</div>

<h3 class="koan-title" id="47"><a href="#47"><span class="koan-num">Case 47</span> Tosotsu’s Three Barriers <span class="koan-title-zh" lang="zh">四十七　兜率三關</span></a></h3>

<div class="koan-case">

Tosotsu Etsu Oshō set up three barriers for his disciples:

1\. You leave no stone unturned to explore profundity, simply to see into your true nature. Now, I want to ask you, just at this moment, where is your true nature?

2\. If you realize your true nature, you are free from life and death. Tell me, when your eyesight deserts you at the last moment, how can you be free from life and death?

3\. When you set yourself free from life and death, you should know your ultimate destination. So when the four elements separate, where will you go?

<p class="orig" lang="zh">兜率悦和尚、設三關問學者、撥草參玄只圖見性。即今上人性在甚處。識得自性方脱生死。眼光落時、作麼生脱。脱得生死便知去處、四大分離向甚處去。</p>

</div>

<div class="koan-comment">

<p class="koan-label rs-label">Mumon’s comment</p>

If you can put turning words to these three questions, you are the master wherever you may stand and command Zen whatever circumstances you may be in. If otherwise, listen: gulping down your meal will fill you easily, but chewing it well can sustain you.

<p class="orig" lang="zh">無門曰、若能下得此三轉語、便可以隨處作主遇縁即宗。其或未然、麁飡易飽、細嚼難飢。</p>

</div>

<div class="koan-verse rs-verse">

<p class="koan-label rs-label">Mumon’s verse <span lang="zh">頌曰</span></p>

This moment’s thoughts sees through eternal time;  
Eternal time is just this moment.  
If you see through this moment’s thought,  
You see through the man who sees through this moment.

<p class="orig" lang="zh">一念普觀無量劫<br />無量劫事即如今<br />如今覻破箇一念<br />覻破如今覻底人</p>

</div>

<h3 class="koan-title" id="48"><a href="#48"><span class="koan-num">Case 48</span> Kempō’s One Road <span class="koan-title-zh" lang="zh">四十八　乾峰一路</span></a></h3>

<div class="koan-case">

A monk asked Kempō Oshō, “It is written, ‘Bhagavats in the ten directions. One straight road to Nirvana.’ I still wonder where the road can be.” Kempō lifted his staff, drew a line, and said, “Here it is.” Later the monks asked the same question to Unmon, who held up his fan and said, “This fan jumps up to the thirty-third heaven and hits the nose of the deity Sakra Devanam Indra. When you strike the carp of the eastern sea, the rain comes down in torrents.”

<p class="orig" lang="zh">乾峰和尚、因僧問、十方薄伽梵、一路涅槃門。未審路頭在甚麼處。峰拈起拄杖、劃一劃云、在者裏。後僧請益雲門。門拈起扇子云、扇子𨁝跳上三十三天、築著帝釋鼻孔。東海鯉魚、打一棒雨似盆傾。</p>

</div>

<div class="koan-comment">

<p class="koan-label rs-label">Mumon’s comment</p>

One, going to the bottom of the sea, lifts up clouds of dust; the other, on the top of the highest mountain, rises towering waves to wash the sky. One holding fast, the other letting go, each stretches out his hand to support the profound teaching. They are just like two riders starting from opposite ends of the course and meeting in the middle. But none on earth can be absolutely direct. When examined with a true eye, neither of these two great masters knows the road.

<p class="orig" lang="zh">無門曰、一人向深深海底行、簸土揚塵、一人於高高山頂立、白浪滔天。把定放行、各出一隻手扶竪宗乗。大似兩箇馳子相撞著。世上應無直底人。正眼觀來二大老惣未識路頭在。</p>

</div>

<div class="koan-verse rs-verse">

<p class="koan-label rs-label">Mumon’s verse <span lang="zh">頌曰</span></p>

Before a step is taken, the goal is reached;  
Before the tongue is moved, the speech is finished.  
Though each move is ahead of the next,  
There is still a transcendent secret.

<p class="orig" lang="zh">未擧歩時先已到<br />未動舌時先説了<br />直饒著著在機先<br />更須知有向上竅</p>

</div>

<h3 class="koan-title" id="postscript"><a href="#postscript">Mumon’s postscript <span class="koan-title-zh" lang="zh">後序</span></a></h3>

<div class="koan-prose">

The sayings and doings of the Buddha and the patriarchs have been set down in their original form. Nothing superfluous has been added by the author, who has taken the lid off his head and exposed his eyeballs. Your direct realization is demanded; it should not be sought through others. If you are a man of realization, you will immediately grasp the point at the slightest mention of it. There is no gate for you to go through; there are no stairs for you to ascend. You pass the checkpoint, squaring your shoulders, without asking permission of the keeper.

<p class="orig" lang="zh">從上佛祖垂示機縁、據款結案、初無剰語。掲翻腦蓋、露出眼睛。肯要諸人直下承當、不從佗覓。若是通方上士。纔聞擧著、便知落處。了無門戸可入、亦無階級可升。掉臂度關不問關吏。</p>

Remember Gensha’s saying, “No-gate is the gate of emancipation; no-meaning is the meaning of the man of the Way.” And Hakuun says, “Clearly you know how to talk of it, but why can’t you pass this simple, specific thing?”

<p class="orig" lang="zh">豈不見玄沙道、無門解脱之門、無意道人之意。又白雲道、明明知道只是者箇、爲甚麼透不過。</p>

However, all this kind of talk is like making a mud pie with milk and butter. If you have passed the Mumonkan, you can make a fool of Mumon. If not, you are betraying yourself. It is easy to know the Nirvana mind but difficult to attain the wisdom of differentiation. When you have realized this wisdom, peace and order will reign over your land.

<p class="orig" lang="zh">恁麼説話、也是赤土搽牛嬭。若透得無門關、早是鈍置無門。若透不得無門關、亦之辜負自己。所謂、涅槃心易曉、差別智難明。明得差別智、家國自安寧。</p>

The change of era to Jōtei [1228], five days before the end of summer session  
Respectfully inscribed by Mumon Ekai Bhikkhu, eighth in succession from Yōgi

<p class="orig" lang="zh">時紹定改元解制前五日<br />楊岐八世孫無門比丘慧開謹識。</p>

Wu-wen kuan (Mumonkan)  
End of the book

<p class="orig" lang="zh">無門關巻終</p>

</div>

## About this translation

The translation is by Katsuki Sekida (1893–1987), a Japanese Zen teacher and the author of *Zen Training* (1975). It comes from his *Two Zen Classics: Mumonkan and Hekiganroku*, “translated with commentaries by Katsuki Sekida; edited and introduced by A. V. Grimstone” (Weatherhill, New York, 1977; reissued by Shambhala, 2005). Only the translation is given here, from the online copy described below; Sekida’s commentaries on each case are in the book. The words in square brackets are his.

The Chinese text follows a Japanese edition of the *Mumonkan* published by Iwanami Bunko, and keeps that edition’s Japanese forms of some characters. Where the online copy had empty boxes for characters it could not display, they have been filled from the Taishō canon, and its slips of transcription corrected (see Source and licence).

What is here is the core of the book: Wumen’s preface and verse, the forty-eight cases, and his postscript. Printed editions often add more around it, including an introduction by the first publisher, a dedication to the emperor, the “Zen Warnings” attributed to Wumen and a forty-ninth case added in 1246. Those are not in the source and are not given here.
