# The opening line is a door, not a plaque

Date: 2026-11-21. Track: landing (the home page).

The first home page opened on "The Supreme Way is not difficult / If only you do not pick and choose." with the author and translator beneath. A fresh critic read it as a quotation on a wall: the most-quoted couplet in Zen, complete in itself, with a caption. The rewrite opens on the Shodoka's "There is the leisurely one, / Walking the Tao, beyond philosophy," and the line beneath says "The first lines of the *Shodoka*, a Zen song. The rest takes twenty minutes."

- Choose an opening that is unfinished: a person in it, a comma at the end, so the only way to complete the sentence is to go in. A famous, self-contained line is a plaque however it is set.
- The line beneath is not an attribution; it is the door. Name the text, say what kind of thing it is, say how long the rest takes, and make the title the link.
- Do not repeat the shelf in the sentence above it. The intro named six texts that sat in the shelf directly below; one phrase ("from Zen poems to the Stoics") does the work.
- Say "passage", not "line": prose texts link by section or entry. And give reading times in the edition's own words ("about forty minutes", "four or five hours"); a formula gave "about 235 min", which no one says and no edition does.
