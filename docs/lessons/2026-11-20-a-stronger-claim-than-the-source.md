# The usual fact error is a claim one notch stronger than its source

Date: 2026-11-20. Track: editorial, from the fact check of the eight introductions (31 online claims and the translators' own words).

Almost nothing was wrong outright. What the check found was stronger wording than the source: "dropped out of the Chinese canon" where the source says the canon stopped including it; "first printed in a book for chanting" where the source says later reprinted in one; a traditional lifespan (778–897) as plain dates; a story from the *Transmission of the Lamp* as biography; a name copied from a web page with its typo ("Stout" for Strout). One claim had no source at all beyond a Wikisource tag (a 1932 translation's US copyright renewal).

**Lesson:** when checking, compare the strength of the verb and the hedge, not only the date. "Traditionally", "the story told of him", "the canon left it out" cost nothing. Check names against a catalogue, not the page you copied them from.
