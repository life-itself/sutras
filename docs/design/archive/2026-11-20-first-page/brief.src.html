A look for Sutras that is the thing it shows

Rufus's direction: “we want to be zen, to be the tao somehow in what we are building.” Stripped down, like Reports That Matter and Co–Star “but a little warmer”; “a little more Plum Village”, though that “can get a little twee”. And: Reports That Matter “has a pretty sober version, you could follow that… I think we do want our distinctive design here, and that often takes real work.” So sober is the floor and distinctive is the aim.

This page gives a feel to judge by, what the admired sites measure, a mood board, and three directions set with Faith in Mind. One is recommended.

1. Is this the feel?“Without lack, without excess”, or one of two others.
2. Which direction?A, B, C or a blend. C is recommended. This is the main thing to look at.
3. The smaller callsSix, at the end, each with a default.

The feel

One phrase that every later choice is tested against. Three candidates; the first is recommended.

Without lack, without excess

Recommended. A line from Faith in Mind itself: “The Way is perfect like great space, / Without lack, without excess.” It works as a test in both directions. Would the reader miss this? If not, it goes. Is the reader missing something? Then it is added, plainly.

A plain room with good light

Warmer, and closer to Plum Village. It gives a picture, where the first gives a test, so it is easier to feel and harder to apply. It also invites furnishing the room.

Ordinary mind

From the Mumonkan, case 19: “Ordinary mind is the Way.” It gives licence to be plain, but says nothing about when something is missing.

Space
Space is the material. Calm and hierarchy come from where things sit and how much room they have, before size, rule or colour.
One hand
The only personality on the page is the reader's marks. Warmth comes from that, never from decoration.
Waiting
The page waits. Nothing moves, pops up or asks. It reads the same on the hundredth visit.
Character
A well-made paperback that someone has read with a pencil. Plain-spoken, unhurried, a little dry.
Not
A temple gift shop (lotus, ensō, brush script). A wellness app (pastel gradient, “start your journey”). A scholar's apparatus (toolbars, panels, tabs). A tasteful AI landing page (cream, terracotta, tracked capitals, three cards).

Before: the site today

The stand-in theme is Flowershow's default. It is clean, and nothing in it knows it is holding a poem.

  • The headline is a filename. “Faith in Mind by Seng Ts'an translated by Master Sheng-yen” is one 59.5px bold sans heading. Title, author and translator are not told apart.
  • Small and wide. Body text is 16px on 28px in a 672px column, about 89 characters a line. Every admired reading page below is 18 to 22px.
  • The poem is a long way down. A source link, a preface and an introduction come first. The stranger who searched for the text meets a biography.
  • No numbers, no notes. A stanza cannot be cited or linked, and nothing of Rufus is on the page.
  • The home page is a 60px headline over six bullet links.

Later the same day the live page showed an “Error parsing MDX” message in place of the text (a build in progress in another workstream), so there is no phone or verse-close-up shot of the old page.

Type

Every one sets its text in a single book-weight serif and keeps a second voice for everything else. The second voice is where each gets its character: Co–Star's mono, Plum Village's handwriting, SuttaCentral's grey small capitals. None gets it from the text face.

Size

The pages that feel generous are 20 to 22px (Plum Village, Craig Mod, Tufte, Sefaria). The 16px pages are the archives. Take 19 to 20px on a laptop, 17 to 18px on a phone.

Measure

Prose runs 70 to 85 characters, wider than the textbook 66. The calmest page here, SuttaCentral, is 56. Take about 65 to 70 for prose, and let verse set its own width from its longest line (50 characters in Faith in Mind).

Line height

1.4 to 1.6 at 20px. Only the 16px pages go to 1.75, to make up for the size. Take 1.5 to 1.6.

Space

Co–Star and Reports That Matter use no rules, boxes or shadows; things are parted by distance. SuttaCentral sets its title small and grey and leaves a clear pause before the first line. Space does the work of hierarchy.

Colour

All the grounds sit within a hair of white, and none of the reading pages uses colour inside the text. Co–Star's mid-grey ink is for short marketing lines; Reports That Matter went to #252525 for reading. Warmth in the admired pages is never a palette. At Plum Village it is a handwritten name and people's faces; at SuttaCentral a faint tint.

Board: verse on screen

What verse needs

Lines left aligned, never centred. A block narrower than prose, set by the longest line. Line height 1.5 to 1.6. One line of space between stanzas, and nothing else between them.

Numbers

Outside the block, small. SuttaCentral hides them until asked; dhammatalks puts them after the verse. Ours need to be seen, because the stanza number is the link (/faith-in-mind#3), so: in the left margin, in the second voice.

The turnover

On a 390px phone at 17 to 18px about 38 to 42 characters fit, and Faith in Mind has lines of up to 50. A turned line hangs in by 1.5em so it cannot be mistaken for a new line. Every card below shows one.

Board: notes in the margin

What a margin note needs

About 0.8 to 0.85 of the text size, tighter lines, a margin of 13 to 16rem, and its first line level with the line it is about. Told apart by at least two of size, face, colour and position.

The mark on the text

None of these marks the passage itself; they rely on a superscript or on position. For a reader's notes on verse the mark matters, since it says “he stopped here”. This is where each direction below differs most.

On a phone

There is no margin. Rufus's notes are few, so they are shown in place under the line, indented, with no tap needed.

Directions

Three readings of the feel, one from each pole in the brief: take away, the warm hand, the inherited book. They differ in where the column sits, how hierarchy is made, and above all in how a line Rufus has marked is shown.

Every specimen sets the same real lines of Faith in Mind (Sheng-yen's translation, as in the repo). The “in brief” line and the two notes are written by the agent to show the form; they are illustrative, not Rufus's. The cards are the phone layout, where the longer lines have to turn over. On a phone the three share one skeleton, because there is one column and no margin; they part more at desk width, below.

In brief. The Way is not hard. What makes it hard is choosing: for and against, love and hate. Stop choosing and it is already here.

The Supreme Way is not difficult

Neither love nor hate,

And you will clearly understand.

If you want the Way to appear,

Pole Take away.

Type Spectral, one size, one weight. Nothing else.

Signature: one size. Title, text, note and navigation are all set at the same size. Hierarchy is made only by space, position and two tones of grey. No accent colour and no mark on the text: a note sits by its line, and that is the mark.

Says Do not pick and choose. Nothing on the page is louder than anything else.

Risk Reads as unfinished to a stranger. A long prose text loses its signposts, and a note in prose has nothing to show which words it is about. Closest of the three to Reports That Matter, so it is the floor more than the aim.

Pole The warm hand.

Type Alegreya for the text, its italic for the notes, Alegreya Sans for the few labels.

Signature: the pen line. A marked passage is underlined in blue-black, as with a fountain pen, and on a wide screen the line runs out of the verse to the note in the margin. The note is in italic, in the same ink.

Says Someone has read this before you and left the book open on the table.

Risk The nearest to twee: a calligraphic face and “handwritten” notes can turn cosy. A blue underline also looks like a link. Structurally it is Tufte CSS with a warmer face, so the pen line carries the distinctiveness alone.

Pole The inherited book.

Type Gentium Book Plus, with Noto Serif TC for Chinese. Gentium is what Plum Village sets its sutras in.

Signature: the red dot. Black is the text. Red does one job: it is the apparatus, meaning the stanza numbers and a small dot in the margin beside whatever Rufus has marked. Red numbering is the scribe's rubric. The dot is loosely after 圈點, the small marks Chinese readers brushed beside passages they valued; ours is one per line in the margin, where theirs ran beside each character.

Says Many hands have been over this text before yours. Here is one more.

Risk Pastiche if the Chinese is overused or the red spreads. Red on off-white sits near the terracotta-on-cream habit, so the ground stays neutral and the red stays small. In prose the dot shows where a marked passage starts, not how far it runs.

Draws on The Doves Press Bible's black and red; woodblock sutra pages; Plum Village's sutra pages; 84000's numbers in the margin.

At desk width, with the notes in the margin

The title block, the first stanza complete and the start of the second, then a marked sentence from Seneca's On the Shortness of Life (Basore's translation, as in the repo) to test each mark in prose. Below about 800px these fall back to the phone layout.

Recommendation: C, held to A's restraint

A is the sober floor and could ship tomorrow. C is the more distinctive, and the one I would build.

  • Its mark comes from the texts' own world and does a job. A red dot in the margin is close to how these texts were marked by people who read them, and it is the smallest way to show what Sutras promises: one reader's hand beside the text.
  • It answers “warmer” without a warm palette. The ground is as neutral as Co–Star's. The warmth is a few points of red.
  • Its parts are shapes. The rule, the vertical title and the dots still read with the colour taken away. So does B's pen line. A, without its tones, is a serif on off-white.
  • It should carry to Seneca. Red numbering is the Western scribe's habit as much as the Chinese printer's, so nothing changes for Meditations; the Chinese title simply does not appear on a text that has none. The weak point is the prose mark: a dot shows where a marked passage begins, not where it ends. That needs solving in the build.

From A I would keep the discipline: one size for everything except the title, and the test of taking away. If C feels like costume to Rufus, the fallback is A with C's dots in grey.

Decisions needed

Each has a default. If there is no answer, the default is built.

Is the feel “Without lack, without excess”?

Recommended: yes. It is from the first text on the site and it tests in both directions.

Which direction?

Recommended: C, with A's restraint. Fallback: A, with C's dots in grey.

Does Chinese appear on the page?

Recommended: the title and author's name only, on texts that have a Chinese original. The full original beside the translation is an edition question for later, not a design one.

Is the text near-black, or Co–Star's mid-grey?

Recommended: near-black (#161514). Mid-grey suits a few lines; over an hour it tires. Reports That Matter made the same change.

Are notes shown at once on a phone, or on a tap?

Recommended: shown at once, under the line. The notes are few, and a page that waits should not hide things.

Pictures?

Recommended: none for now. Later, at most one public-domain image per text (an ink painting, a woodcut), as at Plum Village and Lotsawa House.
