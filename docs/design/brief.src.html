Sutras: five directions

A curated library of wisdom texts: Zen poems, sutras, the Dhammapada, Seneca, Marcus Aurelius, made into editions to read slowly, with Rufus's notes in the margin.

The page should feel as the texts feel: plain, unhurried, exact, with nothing to sell. One look has to hold the Shodoka and Seneca without dressing up as either. Sober is the floor and distinctive is the aim; “a little warmer” is what this board is meant to define. Five directions follow, one image each, then the two references Rufus named as the floor, a few details worth stealing, three things we are not, the site today, and a shortlist.

The feel, to judge each image by
  • Be the thing. The page feels as the texts feel.
  • The text is the design. Everything else is quieter than the text.
  • Space is the material. Hierarchy from room before size, rule or colour.
  • Something to look at twice. One moment of real craft on every page. Restraint alone is not beauty.
  • It waits. Nothing moves, pops up or asks.
  • The notes are a hand, Rufus's, never mistakable for the text.
  • Not: a temple gift shop, a wellness app, a scholar's apparatus, a tasteful AI landing page, or Garamond on off-white with grey numerals.

Five directions

1

Ink and space

For Sutras: most of the page empty, and what is on it made with care. A few lines of verse where Sesshū has a few strokes; a short row of texts on the home page where Kaikado has a row of caddies; one small brushed mark and nothing else. Zen as emptiness, and the most Zen idea on this board.

the proportion of empty to full; a grey ground rather than white; things in a row with room between them; one hand-made mark. any attempt to draw the mountain ourselves. The ink is the texts' job; ours is the space around them.
2

The warm page

For Sutras: warm grey paper, one piece of quiet art per text, a small label, and then the poem. Warmth from the ground and from an image, not from a palette. The nearest thing on the web to our texts, already carrying Red Pine.

the ground; an old landscape with one modern stroke across it, as the one image a text gets; the long breath before the first line. a full screen of art before every page, the magazine nav, and the sleekness that Seeds of Renaissance's own feel already warns about.
3

The hand

For Sutras: warmth from a person rather than a palette: one brushed name, a woodcut, a line in brown italic, and the text at once. The most human page here.

a brushed name as the only ornament; “Sutras / Discourse on Love” with the source line under it; the text starting straight away in a generous serif. the Play and Download buttons above the first line, the picture floated into the text, and the yellows of the rest of the site. Twee begins one step past here.
4

Red and black

For Sutras: black text as one continuous surface, and red for nothing but the marks around it: the seals of the readers who came before on the scroll, one initial on the Doves page. Our stanza numbers and Rufus's notes would be the red. East and West agree on this page, which is why it can hold Seneca.

red with one job and no other colour; the text block given its full width and left alone; the seals as the model for notes, since they are literally the marks of earlier readers. copying either page. Vertical text, a seal-script wordmark or a scroll layout would be costume; so would anything that looks antique.
5

Seeds of Renaissance, adapted

For Sutras: Rufus's suggestion: take the family system and tweak it. Keep its ink and paper, its hairline rules, its rule that red is rare, its headings and its italic; swap the sans it reads in for a serif; and leave out everything that belongs to a movement rather than a library. The quiet room in the same house.

the paper and ink; red reserved for the apparatus (there, links and footnote marks; here, numbers and notes); the display face for the site's name only; the heading and italic faces; four faces already licensed. the night sections, the pale yellow, torn edges, woodcut swallows, collage photographs, paper grain, and capitals anywhere but the name. The risk is that Sutras reads as a sub-brand, and that is a question for Rufus below.

Shortlist

A · Red and black, held to ink and space

Direction 4 with direction 1's restraint: black text, red apparatus, most of the page empty. The most distinctive, and from the texts' own world. Risk: costume.

B · The warm page, with the hand

Directions 2 and 3: warm paper, one image or one brushed mark per text, then the text. The plainest answer to “warmer”. Risk: tasteful and sleek.

C · Seeds of Renaissance, adapted

Direction 5: the family system with a reading serif and the movement's imagery removed. The least work and the clearest family tie. Risk: a sub-brand.

D · Ink and space alone

Direction 1 on its own: grey ground, a brushed mark, the text, nothing else. The purest, and the easiest to get dull. Risk: Standard Ebooks with a nicer grey.

Recommendation: take A and B to specimens, side by side, with real lines from Faith in Mind and Seneca. Add C only if Rufus wants Sutras to read as part of the Seeds of Renaissance family. D is the restraint A is held to rather than a third specimen.
