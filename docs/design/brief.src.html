A look for Sutras that is the thing it shows

A curated library of wisdom texts: Zen poems, sutras, the Dhammapada, Seneca, Marcus Aurelius, made into editions to read slowly, with Rufus's notes in the margin.

The brief in three lines. The page should feel as the texts feel: plain, unhurried, exact, with nothing to sell. One look has to hold the Shodoka and Seneca without dressing up as either. Sober is the floor (Reports That Matter), distinctive is the aim, and “a little warmer” is still to be defined: this board is meant to define it. Full brief: docs/design/brief.md.

Eight references below, each standing for a different direction, each with what to take and what to leave. Then a few details worth stealing, three things we are not, and a shortlist. Fifty-odd other candidates were looked at and are listed in the repo, not here.

The feel, to judge each image by
  • Be the thing. The page feels as the texts feel.
  • The text is the design. Everything else is quieter than the text.
  • Space is the material. Hierarchy from room before size, rule or colour.
  • Something to look at twice. One moment of real craft on every page. Restraint alone is not beauty.
  • It waits. Nothing moves, pops up or asks.
  • The notes are a hand, Rufus's, never mistakable for the text.
  • Not: a temple gift shop, a wellness app, a scholar's apparatus, a tasteful AI landing page, or Garamond on off-white with grey numerals.

Before

The site today: Flowershow's default, tidied. Clean, and nothing in it knows it is holding a poem.

Eight directions, one image each

Each image was chosen because it is beautiful first and relevant second, and captured at the moment that matters, which is often not the top of the page. Museum objects are open-licence and credited.

1

The empty room

For Sutras: a page that is almost nothing. One typeface doing everything, the text, hierarchy by distance alone, grey on near-white (or ash on black for a single screen). Zen read as taking away.

the nerve to leave the page empty; three objects given a whole screen; one face for name, text and labels; texture from a faint line drawing, never from colour. the cold, the centring, the knowing tone and the 14px text. It is a poster for an app, not a page to read for an hour.
2

The sober document

For Sutras: the text treated as a document. Title, source line and text share one left edge on a grey ground; a photograph only where a text has one. This is the floor Rufus named: every other direction has to be at least this good.

one left edge; kicker, title and text on it; nothing boxed; a long text given weight and air; the ¶ link in the margin. the mono labels and the investigative chill; the sans for reading. Right for a report, cold for a poem.
3

The warm page

For Sutras: warm grey paper, a small-caps title, an italic line for poet and translator, the Chinese above the English, verse given a 50-character measure with air around it, and one piece of quiet art per text. The nearest thing on the web to our page, already carrying Song dynasty poems and Red Pine's Cold Mountain.

the ground (warm, not cream); the sequence of title, attribution, Listen, standfirst, text; Chinese and English set as equals; a drawn artwork opening a text, printed through the paper rather than pasted on. the Store and Engage in the nav; a full screen of art before every piece (one per text, not per page); the slight sleekness that the Seeds of Renaissance feel already warns about.
4

The hand

For Sutras: warmth from a person rather than a palette: one brushed title, a woodcut, a line in brown italic, and the text at once at 20px. Where “warmer” most plainly lives.

the brushed name as the only ornament on the page; “Sutras / Discourse on Love” with the source under it; the text starting straight away in a generous serif. the Play and Download buttons above the first line; the picture floated into the text; the yellows and peaches of the rest of the site. This is where twee begins, one step past here.
5

Red and black: the inherited page

For Sutras: the forms these texts came down in: ruled columns, black text as one continuous surface, and red for nothing but the marks of readers and owners. Our stanza numbers and Rufus's marks would be the red; the text stays black. Zen read as lineage.

text as a single block in a ruled frame; red for the apparatus and nothing else; the seals as the trace of the readers who came before, which is exactly what margin notes are. copying it. Vertical text, a scroll layout or a seal-script wordmark would be costume. The open question is whether this page can hold Seneca; the next direction is its Western half.
6

The pocket classic

For Sutras: the Western inheritance for Seneca and Marcus: a small book made to be carried and read anywhere, a narrow measure, the italic as a text face, one initial, and a wide margin that invites a pencil. The companion to direction 5 rather than a rival to it.

the measure; italic as a voice for the whole of a short text or for the notes; the outer margin as part of the page; one initial per text and no other ornament. the painted border and anything that looks antique. The idea, not the look. Seeds of Renaissance's rule applies here too: nothing may look backwards.
7

The text as object

For Sutras: each text is an edition with a cover: one colour, spaced capitals, author and title and nothing else. The home page is a shelf of them, the EPUB is the same object, and the reading page is the inside of the book.

one colour per series (blue for fiction, white for essays: for us, perhaps one for the Buddhist texts and one for the Stoics); author in small caps above, title in spaced caps; a house style that never changes. the shop: basket, breadcrumbs, photographs of books in rooms. A cover grid is a bookshop and Sutras is a reading room, so this is a home-page idea inside another direction rather than a direction on its own.
8

Seeds of Renaissance, adapted

For Sutras: Rufus's suggestion: borrow the family system and tweak it. Ink and paper grounds, rigour as the form, hairline rules, red kept rare, Polyamine for the site title only, Restora for headings and the poetic italic, and a reading serif where Seeds uses Bricolage. The imagery of a movement removed, leaving the quiet of a library.

the grounds and inks (paper #fbfaf6, ink #1b1916); “red is rare” (there: links and footnote marks; here: stanza numbers and Rufus's marks); Apfel Grotezk labels at 11–14px; Restora for headings and italic lines; the plates-and-captions discipline, turned on the apparatus; four faces already licensed and self-hosted. the reading face: Bricolage Grotesque is a sans with no italic, and a wisdom text wants a serif (Restora at 19px is one candidate, an open serif another). Drop the night section, the sunlight yellow, torn edges, woodcut swallows, collage photographs and paper grain: those are a movement's voice. Polyamine capitals for the site name alone, never a title. Sutras reads as a Seeds of Renaissance sub-brand. Whether that family resemblance is wanted is Rufus's call, and the main question this direction asks.

Shortlist

Three to take to specimens, with the sober document as the floor under all of them. Each would be shown as the first screen of Faith in Mind on a phone and the same stanza at desk width with a margin note, plus a few lines of Seneca.

A · The warm page

Direction 3, with the hand (4) as its source of warmth: Emergence's paper and sequence, one brushed or drawn element per text. Nearest to “a little warmer”, and already proven on Chinese and English verse. Risk: tasteful and sleek.

B · Red and black

Direction 5 held to the empty room's (1) restraint, with the pocket classic (6) as its Western half: black text, red apparatus, ruled space, an italic for the notes. The most distinctive, from the texts' own world. Risk: costume, and holding Seneca.

C · Seeds of Renaissance, adapted

Direction 8 as described: the family system with a reading serif and the imagery removed. The least work and the clearest family tie. Risk: Sutras reads as a sub-brand.

Not shortlisted: the text as object (7), which becomes the home page inside whichever direction wins; the sober document (2), which is the measure rather than a candidate.

For Rufus

Which of A, B and C go to specimens?

Recommended: all three, side by side, since they answer “warmer” in three different ways. Default if no answer: A and B.

Is the Seeds of Renaissance family resemblance wanted?

If yes, C becomes the floor and A or B are tweaks to it. If no, C drops. Default: C stays in as one of three.

Where does “warmer” live for you?

Paper and a hand (3, 4), or red on black (5)? One reaction to each image above is more useful than a verdict.

Three things you find beautiful that are not here.

On paper or screen. They would correct this board faster than anything else. Default: your ux-patterns archive stands in.
