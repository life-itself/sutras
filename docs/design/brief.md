# Sutras — design brief

Status: draft 3, 2026-11-20, after a fresh-agent critique. Sparse on purpose: it grows with Rufus's reactions to the mood board. The first design page was rejected ("mood board is weak. where is the true beauty in this"): it batched feel, research, board and directions on one page, chose screenshots for relevance rather than beauty, and let measurements crowd the page. This brief and the board that follows it are the restart.

## Goal

A look for Sutras that *is* the thing it shows. Rufus: "we want to *be* zen, to be the tao somehow in what we are building." The first screen shows words from a teaching, not a description of a website, and the page looks made by someone who reads these texts slowly.

The central problem: one look has to hold the Shodoka and Seneca, a Zen poem and a Stoic letter, without dressing up as either. That is what makes this hard and what can make it distinctive.

The bar: Reports That Matter "has a pretty sober version, you could follow that… I think we do want our distinctive design here, and that often takes real work." Sober is the floor. Distinctive is the aim.

## Who it is for

1. Rufus, reading slowly and returning: *Faith in Mind* on a phone in the evening, *Meditations* on a laptop with his notes open. The first and permanent reader.
2. Someone who searched for a text and lands here cold. They should want to read it here rather than elsewhere.

## The kinds of page

Verse (*Faith in Mind*, Shodoka, Dhammapada), prose (Seneca, *Meditations*), cases (Mumonkan, with Chinese), a text's front matter, Rufus's notes beside the text, and a home page that is a place to pause on a short list. The test page is *Faith in Mind* on a phone: a direction that does not make it better to read fails whatever else it does. Details of what each page needs are for the specimen stage, in [research-reading-features.md](research-reading-features.md).

## Rufus's references, decoded

- **Reports That Matter**: take the one left edge, the grey ground, nothing boxed, a long document treated as a document. Leave the mono labels and the investigative chill.
- **Co–Star**: take the nerve to leave the page almost empty, one face doing everything, a modest wordmark. Leave the coldness, the centring, the knowing tone.
- **Plum Village**: take the sense that a person made this, and a text given the room of a sutra. Leave the yellow buttons, the peach panels, the carousels: that is where it tips into twee.
- **"Warmer"** is still undefined. The board should show whether warmth lives in the ground and ink, in the type, or in a hand (notes, a brushed title).

## The feel

- **Be the thing.** Plain, unhurried, exact, nothing to sell: the page feels as the texts feel.
- **The text is the design.** Everything else (title, numbers, notes, nav) is quieter than the text.
- **Space is the material.** Hierarchy from position and room before size, rule or colour.
- **Something to look at twice.** Every page has one moment of real craft (a title, a numeral, a turned line, a note in the margin) that rewards a second look. This is the positive test: restraint alone is not beauty.
- **It waits.** Nothing moves, pops up or asks.
- **The notes are a hand**, Rufus's, and never mistakable for the text.
- **Not:** a temple gift shop (lotus, ensō clip-art, brush script); a wellness app; a scholar's apparatus of panels and tabs; a tasteful AI landing page (cream, terracotta, tracked caps, three cards); and the likeliest dull result of this brief, Garamond on off-white with grey numerals, which is Tufte CSS again.

## What is fixed

- Flowershow, restyled. How far the build goes is decided after a direction is picked, not before.
- Light only for now. Serif for the text. Open-licence fonts. The face for Chinese characters is named.
- The texts are the content. No stock photography. A direction may propose one kind of image (for example one public-domain ink work per text) but must stand without it.
- Name, mark and wordmark are parked.

## The mood board: rules

- Images from the web and beyond it: printed pages, calligraphy, sutra scrolls, ink painting, type specimens, ceramics. Roughly half not websites.
- The test for every image: is this truly beautiful, and would Rufus feel something looking at it? Would he keep it on his desk? Relevant-but-plain is dropped.
- Capture the beautiful moment, which is often not the top of the page.
- Each image: take → and leave →. Shown large. Grouped so that three or four directions emerge from the images themselves.
- Prior work to draw on: Rufus's `rufuspollock/ux-patterns` (archive, essay-pages board, typography notes).

## Done-check for this stage

- The brief fits on one screen and Rufus can say what is wrong with it.
- A board of 15 to 25 images meeting the rules above, with 2–3 anti-references and the current site as "before".
- The directions named on the board, one sentence each on what each would mean for Sutras, and one or two recommended as a question.
- Rufus reacts ("these references, these takes") and picks. Only then: 2–3 specimens.

## Open questions

- Where does "warmer" live: ground and ink, type, or a hand?
- How much of the East Asian inheritance (red and black, vertical title, the ruled block) is right for a library that also holds Seneca and Marcus Aurelius?
- Is the home page a list or a place?
- Three things Rufus already finds beautiful, on paper or screen. Default if unanswered: the archive in ux-patterns stands in.

## Rufus's reactions to the first board (2026-11-20, evening)

Quoted, with scope, from his reply to the six-and-two board:

- **Kaikado:** "I love the tea caddies in a row." "Very clean… almost even better than the Co–Star."
- **Emergence:** "I love the emergence magazine, particularly that page that we've got." "If I were following something emergence, this is really very good."
- **Co–Star:** "just very clean. It's very beautiful and clean. I like the fact that it has images a lot. I think the images are something that would add here, of some kind, to a textual site. Charming." But: "I'm not sure I would be using the fonts or other things from it." Then, clarifying: "co-star not just images per se but how they weave into the page and the layout but not sure so relevant for us. so more some inspiration."
- **Fitzcarraldo:** "these look great, potentially for the epubs, or if we produce PDFs." On colour: "Blue can be quite cold. I like the red of the page on emergence, on that particular example, and the scroll behind it, but I also like the blue of the Fitzcarraldo covers."
- **Plum Village:** "not really, not at all. It's not got the beauty and elegance. It's nice, but it's too warm. I don't really buy the Plum Village stuff at all."
- **Zhao Mengfu, the seals:** "not really… I think this takes us too much that way, though. I think having this kind of imagery of scrolls and so on is nice."
- **Funky:** "I think we could go something also a bit funkier… I don't know if we have any funky options." "That would take us in a very different direction."
- "I'm open here."

What follows for the next board: widen towards Emergence and Kaikado (quiet, clean, with images); show what images could do on a textual site (drawn or photographed objects, scroll and ink imagery as pictures rather than as page forms); find a genuinely funky option; set Plum Village and the seals aside; carry the colour question (a warm red versus a cool blue) as a small strip. "A little warmer" now has a ceiling: Plum Village is past it.

## Rufus's reactions to the second board (2026-11-20, night)

- "i didn't really need more emergence. that's not really helpful. i already know that." (Lesson: widen into new territory, not more of the thing he already picked.)
- "i like sesshu."
- "no to making software. not this vibe at all."
- "if i were basing off something it would be that one emergence page of red pine… but the drawing is doing a lot of the work."
- "we don't really have logo or other sense of direction but i could imagine the character for wisdom and we could borrow the gold on indigo or turn it to red on something… just ideas."
- "Overall i think we have a decent first direction… and color inspirations from the red, blue and gold etc."

What follows: the direction is Emergence's Red Pine page as the base, held to Kaikado's restraint, with an ink image of the Sesshū kind doing the work the drawing does there; colour drawn from the warm red, the Fitzcarraldo blue and the gold-on-indigo; a possible mark in a single character for wisdom. No funk. Next stage: specimens.
