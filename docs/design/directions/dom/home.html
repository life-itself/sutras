A library of wisdom texts

Sutras

In brief A small library of wisdom texts, from Buddhist sutras and Zen poems to the Stoics. Each is made into an edition to read slowly: a clean page, a short introduction, a link for every passage, notes in the margin and an EPUB to download. Curated, not complete: each text is here because it is worth reading, and some carry Rufus Pollock’s own notes alongside, starting with Meditations.

Zen (Ch’an)

Faith in Mind

Attributed to Seng Ts’an · translated by Master Sheng-yen with Paul Kennedy

A short poem on the mind that does not pick and choose.

Shodoka

Attributed to Yung-chia Hsuan-chueh · translated by Robert Aitken and Eido Shimano

The Song of Enlightenment: sixty-four verses on seeing one’s own nature, sung by someone who has woken up.

The Gateless Gate

Wumen Huikai (Mumon Ekai) · translated by Katsuki Sekida

The Mumonkan: forty-eight koans, each with a comment and a verse by Wumen (Mumon), beginning with Jōshū’s “Mu”. With the Chinese.

Mahayana Buddhism

The Diamond Sutra

Translated by Thich Nhat Hanh

The Buddha and Subhuti on freeing all beings while holding on to no idea of self, of beings, or even of the teaching.

The Diamond Sutra, Red Pine’s translation

Translated by Red Pine (Bill Porter)

The same sutra from the Sanskrit, keeping its repetitions and its full closing verse.

Early Buddhism

The Dhammapada

Translated by Thanissaro Bhikkhu

423 short verses from the Pāli Canon, attributed to the Buddha, on the mind and the path.

Stoicism

On the Shortness of Life

Seneca · translated by John W. Basore

“It is not that we have a short space of time, but that we waste much of it.”

Meditations

Marcus Aurelius · translated by Gregory Hays

A Roman emperor’s notes to himself on how to live, with Rufus Pollock’s notes in the margin.
