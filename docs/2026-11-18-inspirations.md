# Inspirations for re-visioning Sutras

Purpose: feed a visioning session, then a moodboard. Covers what each site is for and how it looks. Rufus wants Sutras to become something he can learn wisdom work and practice from, perhaps structured like a knowledge base (concept pages, people, works, as in Wilber Wiki).

Provenance: organism.earth, Lotsawa House, 84000, Standard Ebooks and ttc.tasuki.org were read directly in this pass (HTML or page text). Sefaria, Rigpa Wiki and Treasury of Lives blocked automated fetches; those entries and the other sites rely on prior knowledge of the sites, so check visuals before they go on the moodboard. No screenshots were taken (no screenshot tool used). Design details marked "(from CSS)" were read from the page source.

## Patterns across these

- The best sites treat a text as a hub, not a page: the work links to its author, topics, mentions, parallels and commentary (Sefaria, SuttaCentral, organism.earth). A "work, person, concept" triple is the common skeleton, and it maps directly onto Wilber Wiki.
- Translations and sources side by side, or switchable on one page, are the single strongest learning aid for wisdom texts (SuttaCentral, ctext.org, ttc.tasuki.org, Quran.com). Sutras already has several translations of some works, so this is a natural fit.
- Short human introductions beat long scholarly apparatus: a paragraph on why this text matters, who wrote it and how to approach it (Lotsawa House, 84000, organism.earth blurbs). Add a "how to read this" or practice note and it becomes a learning path rather than an archive.
- Glossaries work best when inline and hover or tap based, with the original-language term shown (Perseus word lookup, 84000 glossary, Lotsawa House Sanskrit and Tibetan terms). For Sutras: a small set of concept pages (emptiness, no-mind, ...) that terms link to.
- Reading comfort is a feature in itself: a serif body at generous size, narrow measure, quiet colour, dark mode, and display options for the reader (Standard Ebooks, Quran.com, organism.earth document options). Wisdom texts want slowness, so avoid dense chrome.
- Quotes are a first-class object, not decoration: organism.earth counts and surfaces quotes, and can play them in a "meditation" mode. Verses or koans as shareable, linkable units (one URL per verse or case) suit short wisdom texts.
- Tone splits into two camps: archival and neutral (SuttaCentral, Sefaria, Lotsawa House) versus atmospheric and poetic (organism.earth). The most interesting option for Sutras is an archive-grade structure with a contemplative front door.
- Curated entry points (topics, "start here", teacher or lineage groupings, daily or random passage) matter more than a full catalogue for a small library; none of the strong sites make the reader face a flat list.

## organism.earth (Rufus's example)

URL: https://organism.earth (library at https://organism.earth/library/)

What it is: Two layers. The front door is an immersive full-screen "meditation" (a starfield, video, music, and quotes read aloud by "Gaia", the mind of Earth, each with a portrait, author, work and year). Behind it is The Library of Consciousness, a large curated collection of texts, talks and books on philosophy, mysticism and the evolution of mind, with the tagline "Evolution isn't done yet, it's just getting started."

What it does well:
- Structure: three index types that cross-link. Topics (e.g. Eastern Philosophy 212 documents, Cosmic Consciousness 237, each with a one-sentence definition and a Browse link), Minds (authors) and documents. The nav is minimal: Minds, Quotes, Search, Blog, RSS, About. The home page opens with a rotating quote, which sets tone before any catalogue.
- Author pages: portrait, role ("Philosopher"), a short bio credited to Wikipedia, then a filterable and sortable list of that person's documents. Filters by format (text, audio, video) and sorts by date, duration, word count and popularity. Each entry shows date, format, host, word count, reading time ("about 36 minutes"), quote count, views, plus a blurb and "Mentioned in 69 documents".
- Document pages: title, author, year, a descriptive blurb, topic tags, a "Mentions" list that links to other people, and a link to where the document is hosted (often archive.org). Counters at the top show words, quotes and views. A small options panel: highlight quotes, highlight hyperlinks, scroll with audio.
- Learning aids: topic definitions, mentions and "mentioned in" graph, reading time, curated quotes. No glossary or practice notes; the learning path is implicit.
- Visual tone: space and night-sky. The front page uses Dosis (a thin rounded sans) and Roboto Serif (from CSS); the library uses Alegreya (a humanist serif), Dosis and Roboto Mono, with Font Awesome icons. Dark, cosmic, quiet; the text is the point. Poetic copy ("treasures await if you dare crack open those wacky, weird books").

Idea to borrow: the metadata strip on every work (word count, reading time, quote count, topic tags, "mentioned in N") plus author pages that list works with sort and filter. On Sutras that gives each text a card with reading time, tradition, translator and linked concepts. Also borrow the quote-led home page.

## SuttaCentral

URL: https://suttacentral.net

What it is: The reference library of early Buddhist texts (the Pali canon and parallels in Chinese, Tibetan and Sanskrit), free and non-profit, with translations in many languages.

What it does well:
- Structure: texts are addressed by stable IDs (e.g. MN 10, DN 22) and arranged by collection and sub-collection, with a sidebar of parallels across traditions.
- Reading experience: the "sutta" page can show the Pali root text and one or more translations side by side or line by line, with the original in a switchable column. Settings for text size, serif or sans, and a night theme.
- Learning aids: hover dictionary lookups on Pali words, "Parallels" panel, per-sutta summaries, and an Essays and Guides section for orientation.
- Visual tone: sober, light and uncluttered, close to a well-set book, restrained colour.

Idea to borrow: the line-by-line aligned layout with a toggle for original, translation and a second translation. For Faith in Mind and the Diamond Sutra, pair translation with commentary at the paragraph level.

## Sefaria

URL: https://www.sefaria.org (blocked automated fetch; description from prior knowledge)

What it is: A free, open library of Jewish texts (Torah, Talmud, commentaries) with translations and a deeply linked text graph, plus user "source sheets".

What it does well:
- Structure: every text is split into addressable segments, and each segment can open a "connections" sidebar listing commentary, cross-references, translations, topics and sheets.
- Reading experience: a multi-panel reader. Open the text in one panel, a commentary beside it, and a dictionary or topic in a third. Bilingual toggle (Hebrew, English, both).
- Learning aids: topic pages (people, places, concepts) with definitions and links to source passages, source sheets as curated learning paths, and a lexicon.
- Visual tone: calm, bookish, white background, serif text with large Hebrew type, dark mode, subtle colour coding by category.

Idea to borrow: the connections sidebar on a passage. Click a verse or case and see commentary, related concepts and parallel passages in other works. This is the knowledge-base idea in reading form.

## Lotsawa House

URL: https://lotsawahouse.org

What it is: "The Home of Tibetan Buddhist Texts in Translation": over 6,000 short texts (prayers, verses, practice texts, teachings) from Tibetan and Indian masters, free to read, and downloadable as PDF and EPUB. Run as a community interest company on donations.

What it does well:
- Structure: the library is organised by teacher (Tibetan Masters, Indian Masters, Words of the Buddha), by topic (Dzogchen, Praise, Pilgrimage, and so on), by translation project, and by featured series. There are teacher pages with biographies.
- Reading experience: short pages with the title, translator credit, and a brief introduction before the text. PDFs and EPUBs for reading offline. Interface available in nine languages.
- Learning aids: topic introductions, introductions per text, terms given in Tibetan script, Wylie transliteration and Sanskrit equivalents, a Tools section.
- Visual tone: clean and image-led, with a photograph or artwork per text or project, a dark header with a white logo, and plenty of white space.

Idea to borrow: the three-way browse (by teacher, by topic, by series) for a small library, with a one-paragraph introduction before each text and PDF/EPUB download.

## 84000 Reading Room

URL: https://84000.co/translation

What it is: A non-profit project translating the Tibetan Buddhist canon (Kangyur and Tengyur) into English, with 624 published translations at the time of the page read.

What it does well:
- Structure: texts are arranged by canon section (Discipline, Discourses, Tantra, Dharani, Curated Collection), with catalogue numbers like Toh 569, page counts, publication dates and "New" flags, plus sort, filter and a Finding Aid. The Scholar's Room adds parallel texts and multilingual search.
- Reading experience: each translation has a translator's introduction, a summary, the text with endnotes, and a glossary of named people, places and terms linked from the body (from prior knowledge of the reader).
- Learning aids: introduction plus summary plus glossary per text, and a "Reading Room" framing for general readers separate from the "Scholar's Room".
- Visual tone: clean and light, with flat illustrated icons (a mountain, a wave, fire, a cloud, a tree) for each canon section, and a grid of cards.

Idea to borrow: two doors, one for the general reader (introduction, summary, light notes) and one for the scholar (parallel texts, terms). Also the small flat icon set per category, which suits a moodboard.

## Quran.com

URL: https://quran.com/1

What it is: A free, non-profit reading and study platform for the Quran, with many translations, recitations, and commentary (tafsir).

What it does well:
- Structure: browse by surah, juz, page or topic, with per-verse URLs.
- Reading experience: two modes, "Translation" (verse by verse with the original and a translation underneath) and "Reading" (a clean page layout like a printed book). Per-verse tools: audio, bookmark, tafsir, reflections, copy, share.
- Learning aids: choose from many translations, word-by-word translation, tafsir, community reflections and lessons, and a learning plan and streak features.
- Visual tone: calm and minimal, with a white or sepia or dark theme, large well-set Arabic, and a light green accent.

Idea to borrow: the two-mode reader (study mode with per-verse tools versus pure reading mode), and per-verse "reflections" which could become practice notes for Sutras.

## Chinese Text Project (ctext.org)

URL: https://ctext.org/dao-de-jing

What it is: A large digital library of pre-modern Chinese texts, with translations and an integrated dictionary.

What it does well:
- Structure: per-chapter pages for classics such as the Dao De Jing, with a switch between the original and an English translation, and links to commentaries and a concordance.
- Reading experience: the original Chinese with parallel English, character-level lookup, and the option to show commentary below each line.
- Learning aids: click-through dictionary, concordance showing where the same term appears across the corpus, and links to parallel passages.
- Visual tone: dated and functional, but information-rich; a reminder of the depth to aim for under a more beautiful skin.

Idea to borrow: click a key term in the text and see every other place it occurs across the library (a concordance). For Sutras: "show me every mention of emptiness or no-mind across the five works".

## Tao Te Ching: compare translations (ttc.tasuki.org)

URL: https://ttc.tasuki.org

What it is: A tiny single-purpose viewer that shows up to five translations of the Tao Te Ching side by side, chapter by chapter. The site's own tagline is "There is no escape."

What it does well:
- Structure: one chapter at a time, choose which translations to compare.
- Reading experience: the same verse in several voices at once shows what each translator chose, which teaches the text better than any note.
- Learning aids: the comparison is the learning aid.
- Visual tone: plain, text-only, almost no design; the content carries it.

Idea to borrow: offer "compare" for works with more than one translation. The Diamond Sutra already has two in the repo (diamond.md and diamond-red-pine.md). Note that organism.earth links out to this site for its Tao Te Ching entry.

## Perseus Digital Library and Scaife Viewer

URL: https://scaife.perseus.org

What it is: The classic open library of Greek and Latin texts (including Seneca), with a modern reader (Scaife).

What it does well:
- Structure: texts by author and work, with citation-based addressing (section numbers).
- Reading experience: the Latin or Greek source alongside translation, with word-by-word morphological lookup and dictionary entries on click.
- Learning aids: lexicon and grammar on demand, linked translations and commentaries.
- Visual tone: academic, plain, high-density.

Idea to borrow: citation-based addressing (Seneca, Shortness of Life 3.2) so any passage has a stable, linkable reference, and a lookup popover for key terms.

## Standard Ebooks

URL: https://standardebooks.org

What it is: A volunteer project producing carefully typeset, proofread, free editions of public domain books, in the spirit of well-made paperbacks.

What it does well:
- Structure: a catalogue by author, subject and collection, with a consistent style manual; source files kept in Git so corrections come through pull requests.
- Reading experience: proper typography (curly quotes, correct dashes, hyphenation), semantic markup that gives popup footnotes and navigable tables of contents, original blurbs and encyclopedia links, custom covers from public-domain fine art.
- Visual tone: simple, with a clean header and before-and-after comparison images that show the typographic improvement. The covers are the visual identity.

Idea to borrow: set the standard for typography (quotes, dashes, hyphenation, popup notes) and use public-domain art (for example Japanese or Chinese ink paintings) for a cover per text. A moodboard anchor for reading quality.

## Rigpa Wiki

URL: https://www.rigpawiki.org (blocked automated fetch; description from prior knowledge)

What it is: A MediaWiki-based encyclopedia of Tibetan Buddhism covering teachers, texts, terms, places and practices.

What it does well:
- Structure: entity pages (a person, a text, a term, a place), with infobox-style lead data, category lists and heavy internal links, and Sanskrit and Tibetan names on each page.
- Learning aids: the encyclopedia is the learning aid; each term page defines, gives variants and links to related concepts and sources.
- Visual tone: standard wiki, functional.

Idea to borrow: the people, works and concepts page types, which are exactly what Wilber Wiki already does. Rigpa Wiki shows the shape for a contemplative tradition: a term page with original-language names, a short definition, and "see also".

## Treasury of Lives

URL: https://treasuryoflives.org (blocked automated fetch; description from prior knowledge)

What it is: A biographical encyclopedia of Tibetan and Himalayan lives from Harvard-affiliated scholars.

What it does well:
- Structure: a biography per person with dates, place, lineage and teachers, and a lineage and timeline view across people; teachers link to students and texts.
- Learning aids: the lineage graph is the learning path (who taught whom).
- Visual tone: scholarly and quiet, portraits, light background.

Idea to borrow: a people page with portrait, dates, a short biography and "taught / was taught by" lineage links, tying each text to its author and the line of transmission.

## Bhagavad Gita Supersite (IIT Kanpur)

URL: https://heritage.iitk.ac.in/gitasupersite/ (formerly gitasupersite.iitk.ac.in)

What it is: An academic site presenting each verse of the Bhagavad Gita with many commentaries and translations from different traditions, plus audio. Site content not fully checked in this pass.

What it does well:
- Structure: chapter and verse navigation, with every verse as a unit.
- Learning aids: per verse, the Sanskrit, transliteration, word meanings, translations and classic commentaries (for example Shankara, Ramanuja), so a reader can see how different schools read the same line.
- Visual tone: academic and dense; not beautiful, but a model of per-verse depth.

Idea to borrow: let several commentators speak on one verse, so the reader sees interpretation as plural. For Sutras: for a koan or verse, show two or three readings (Zen master, scholar, Rufus's own practice note).

## Andy Matuschak's notes (digital garden)

URL: https://notes.andymatuschak.org

What it is: A well-known digital garden: evergreen, densely linked notes, presented with sliding stacked panes.

What it does well:
- Structure: atomic notes with titles that are claims, and backlinks on every note.
- Reading experience: clicking a link opens the target in a new pane to the right, keeping the trail of where you came from visible; fast and calm.
- Visual tone: plain, white, serif or system sans, minimal chrome.

Idea to borrow: the stacked-pane navigation for following a thread from a verse to a concept to another work without losing your place. Not a wisdom site, but the best reference for the interaction.

## Shortlist for the moodboard

Top five for the visioning session:
1. organism.earth: the starting point; quote-led, atmospheric, metadata strip, author pages.
2. Sefaria: connections sidebar and multi-panel reading, the best "text as hub".
3. SuttaCentral: aligned root text and translation with hover lookup; archive-grade and calm.
4. Lotsawa House: small-library browse (teacher, topic, series) with an introduction before each text.
5. Standard Ebooks: typography and cover standard for reading quality.
